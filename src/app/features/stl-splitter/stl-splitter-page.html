A planar cut creates two capped exports

Choose X, Y or Z and a percentage through that axis span. The preview shows the cut plane; it is not a Boolean design editor. The server clips triangles, forms cut contours and caps supported contours. It validates the resulting pieces before returning successful exports. Ambiguous contours or a cut touching unsuitable geometry can be rejected rather than filled by guesswork.

Use the generated 20 mm cube, choose Z at 50%, and submit explicitly. The expected geometric spans are 20 × 20 × 10 coordinate units for each half. Download both pieces, reopen them, check the cut surfaces, closure, dimensions and retained features. This records what to verify; it is not a claim that a physical assembly has been printed.

The splitter uploads the selected STL for server processing when you run it. STL has no dependable stored unit, so keep the source unit assumption with the outputs. A capped cut does not add adhesive allowance, alignment pins, sockets or mating clearance. Do not scale pieces independently to force them to fit.

Prefer source CAD when a cut must preserve a load path, avoid a hole, add fasteners or define a repeatable joint. Stop when either downloaded piece loses a required feature or the contour is rejected. Choose a joint and verify the assembly plan before orienting and slicing each part.
