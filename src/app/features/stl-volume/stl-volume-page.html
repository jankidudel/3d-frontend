A volume is only shown when the model is one connected part with no holes, no non-manifold edges and all faces pointing the same way. It does not merge overlapping parts or check for surfaces that cross each other, and it cannot tell whether a hollow inside was intended.

Example: the same cube in different units

The closed 20 mm example cube has 12 triangles and a volume of 8,000 mm³ (8 cm³) when opened as millimetres. Opened as inches, the same file is 508 mm wide and its volume is 25.4³ (about 16,387) times larger. Changing the unit here only changes how the numbers are read; it does not resize or save the model.

A 40 × 30 × 10 block with an 8 mm square passage has no open edges and 11,360 mm³ of material. A missing-face cube has a hole in its surface instead. The 27 October 2026 local processor record shows about 8,000 and 11,360 mm³ for the closed models and a blank volume for the open cube (our server code, run locally). Downloading an example never uploads anything; choose the file above and press Calculate volume when you're ready.

For real filament use, use your slicer

Volume times density gives the weight of a fully solid part. Real prints use walls, top and bottom layers, infill, supports and purge, which a simple percentage cannot capture. Use your slicer's filament total in the production cost calculator. A blank volume gives no weight. An STL cannot tell you how a slicer will split it into walls and infill: the walls vs infill example explains why. Use your slicer's filament total rather than multiplying solid weight by an infill percentage.

For interior design use the cavity workflow; for result interpretation use units and solid-validity diagnosis. Opening locally and submitting to this server calculator are separate actions.
