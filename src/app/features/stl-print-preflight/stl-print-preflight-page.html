How this check works

The report parses the STL once in your browser, counts topology findings, calculates bounds and tests practical right-angle orientations against a 220 × 220 × 250 mm preset. It samples up to 2,000 triangle locations for wall thickness, so narrow features can be missed and sampled values are approximate.

STL does not declare units. Confirm whether the coordinates represent mm, cm, metres or inches before trusting physical measurements. A clean report cannot guarantee a successful print: calibration, material, orientation, supports and slicer settings still matter.

Example workflow

An oversized model with open edges and sampled thin areas should be repaired first, rechecked, then scaled or split only after the mesh is understood. The report points to each relevant tool instead of treating those different problems as one score.

Preflight FAQ

Can a preflight check tell me an STL is printable?

No. It identifies specific mesh, size and sampled-thickness concerns before slicing; it cannot account for your printer setup or use case.

Why are STL units ambiguous?

STL stores coordinates but no reliable unit declaration, so the same numbers can represent very different physical sizes.

Why does a closed mesh matter for volume?

A solid-volume calculation needs a closed, manifold surface. Holes and non-manifold edges make that result unreliable.

Why is wall thickness sampled?

Sampling keeps the browser tool responsive, but it is not an exhaustive measurement of every point on the model.
