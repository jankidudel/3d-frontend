What to do with the result

Result Next action
Some walls are thinner than your limit Look at those spots in the model and in the sliced layers. Compare with your nozzle's line width once you've confirmed the wall really is thin.
No thin walls found Still check important features: the sample can miss small areas, and some points may not have been measurable.
Some points could not be measured Usually holes, zero-area triangles or unclear geometry; check them in CAD and the viewer.
Nothing could be measured Check the unit and the model. This is not a pass, and not a zero.
File could not be analysed Choose a valid, non-empty STL under the size limit and try again.

What the check covers

These are measurements of the model's walls, not a preview of what your slicer will do. For slicer settings, compare walls, top layers and infill in the actual slice.

The checker picks up to 2,000 triangles spread through the file and measures straight inward from the centre of each one to the opposite wall. Zero-area triangles, and points with no opposite wall, are not measured; triangles not picked get no reading. A measurement can reach a different part, so this is neither a per-part result nor a guarantee of the thinnest wall. How the measurement works.

For a bearing housing, check the wall thickness around the seat, including slots and offset walls. That wall is different from the gap between separate parts. This check does not measure clearance, fit, strength or whether a bearing will stay in place, and important areas it didn't sample still need checking.
