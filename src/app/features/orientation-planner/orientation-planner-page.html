What this planner can and cannot decide

It compares six axis-aligned build directions. It does not test every angled rotation, generate supports, predict warping or replace slicer preview. Confirm the chosen orientation with the intended printer, material and slicer settings.

Compare six candidates, then choose for the actual job

The local planner compares six axis directions, nominal bounding-box fit, downward-facing geometry and estimated bed contact. It does not search every arbitrary rotation, generate supports or calculate material strength. STL uses your unit assumption; supported 3MF build geometry is resolved before analysis.

For a hypothetical 100 x 60 x 20 mm box on an 80 x 80 x 120 mm envelope, the original direction exceeds width. Placing the 100 mm axis vertically gives a 60 x 20 mm footprint and nominal fit. That geometric advantage can reduce bed contact and change load direction or support needs.

Keep intended strength, surface finish and support removal as separate decisions. Review the candidate in your slicer, include brim/support allowances and compare the exported orientation with the original. See the orientation procedure for those tradeoffs.
