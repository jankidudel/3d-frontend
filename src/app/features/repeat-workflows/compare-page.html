What this method can and cannot tell you

Deterministic area-distributed points are measured against a triangle acceleration structure in both directions. This is a genuine surface comparison, rather than nearest-vertex matching. The unchanged retriangulation control should give effectively zero distance; the widened-cube example has a 4 mm changed surface.

Finite sampling can miss small features. A result under tolerance does not prove equality, and the sampled maximum is not an upper bound for the whole surface. This is not CAD tolerancing, certified metrology, automatic feature identification or a Boolean solid difference.

Check mesh integrity before slicing, or review a collection with consistent reports. Saved revision projects and cloud history are not available.

Frequently asked comparison questions

Are unchanged dimensions enough?

No. Local holes and lettering can change while outer bounds remain. Interpret each comparison output and inspect critical features.

Does centre alignment correct rotation or units?

No. It translates centres only. Confirm each source unit and any intentional rotation; automatic registration and Boolean difference are not implemented.

Does a low sampled maximum prove equality?

No. It describes finite sampled points, not a certified whole-surface upper bound. Compare the raised-ridge example and recheck topology as well.

Choose a coordinate frame before interpreting distance

Original coordinates compare where the two exports actually sit. Centre alignment translates bounding-box centres only; it does not rotate, rescale or register individual features. A 3 mm translation of an unchanged cube is therefore visible in original coordinates and disappears after centre alignment. Use original coordinates when placement is part of the revision; centre alignment is useful only when translation is irrelevant to your task.

Read three different kinds of result

Bounds show overall spans; topology shows connectivity; sampled distances compare points on each surface against triangles on the other. Equal bounds can hide a removed ridge or changed hole. Equal triangle counts can describe different shapes. A retriangulated planar face can have different triangles yet essentially zero surface distance.

Run the unchanged, retriangulated and widened-cube examples. Keep the same millimetre assumption on each side and original alignment first. The widened export is 24 × 20 × 20 mm rather than 20 × 20 × 20 mm. In original coordinates its moved outer surface is 4 mm away; the sampled maximum describes only measured points, not a guaranteed upper bound everywhere. Changing alignment changes the question, so record it with the tolerance and sampling settings.

If a result looks unexpectedly perfect, inspect small lettering, holes and contact faces manually. Finite area-distributed sampling can miss a tiny feature even when every reported distance is under tolerance. Use source CAD dimensions for design acceptance and review tessellation error when comparing a curve with its mesh approximation.
