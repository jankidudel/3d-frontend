Example: one cube vs two cubes

The one-cube file declares millimetres and has 12 triangles. The two-cube file places the same 20 mm cube twice, at X 0 and X 40 mm. By simple maths, that is 8,000 mm³ for one cube and 16,000 mm³ for two separate cubes.

The 27 October 2026 test result shows about 8,000 mm³ for one cube, but a blank volume for the two-cube build even though it has no mesh problems, because it is two separate parts (our server code, run locally).

Downloading an example does not upload or calculate it. Choose the file above and press Calculate 3MF volume when you're ready.

Reading the result

Check first whether a volume was shown. Blank is not zero, and an error means nothing was measured. When comparing with an STL, compare the same objects and open the STL as millimetres. Adding up separate parts does not merge overlaps or settle whether a hollow was intended.

Volume is the size of the solid shape, not the filament a print will use: slicer settings for walls, top layers and infill are not applied here. See walls vs infill and use your slicer's filament total. For the weight of a solid part, multiply the volume in cm³ by your filament's density; see estimating weight from a 3MF for details. Follow the 3MF volume guide if the volume is blank or looks wrong, then cost the actual sliced job.
