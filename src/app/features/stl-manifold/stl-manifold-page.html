How it works

The checker examines the edges shared by STL triangles and counts open boundaries, non-manifold connections and disconnected shells.

What the results mean

A manifold mesh has a consistent surface around each edge. Open edges and non-manifold edges can confuse slicers; disconnected shells may be intentional but deserve review.

FAQ

What is a manifold mesh?

It is a mesh whose surface joins consistently without ambiguous edge connections.

Are disconnected shells always wrong?

No. Separate parts can be deliberate, but they may need separate printing decisions.

Read edge incidence and orientation separately

One face incident on an edge identifies a boundary; more than two identifies a non-manifold edge. Inconsistent face direction is a separate finding. Several connected shells can be intentional, such as two separate parts, so a shell count alone is not a repair instruction.

The original closed 20 mm cube has 12 triangles and no open edges. The open-face example has 10 triangles and four boundaries. Inspect the missing face before filling it. A zero count does not establish every vertex property or rule out self-intersections.

This checker uploads the chosen STL for server analysis. Results describe detected edge relationships; use the topology explanation to choose a correction, then export and recheck. A successful result is not a printability certificate.
