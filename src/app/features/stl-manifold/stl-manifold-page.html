STL Manifold Checker: Open and Over-Shared Edges

Check whether every edge of your STL joins exactly two triangles. This checker counts open edges and edges shared by three or more faces, the usual causes of non-manifold warnings in slicers, and how many separate pieces the mesh is made of. Use it when you already know the model has a topology warning and want to locate it. It does not detect surfaces that pass through each other.

Check your STL topology

Pressing the button uploads your file for checking on our server (up to 25 MiB). To try it first without uploading your own model, download one of the example files below.

What this check reports

Finding Meaning
One triangle at an edge Open edge: there is a hole or gap here. Check whether the opening was intended.
Two triangles at an edge A normal, properly closed edge. Face direction is checked separately.
Three or more triangles at an edge Non-manifold edge: extra faces meet here, often a duplicate triangle.
Number of pieces How many separate pieces the model is made of (pieces sharing any corner count as one). This is not a count of errors. The STL Repair piece selector groups pieces slightly differently.

The 10-face open cube has four open edges. The 13-face duplicate cube has three non-manifold edges. Two intended closed cubes show as two pieces with no edge problems.

If your report says...

Finding Narrow next action
Boundary edges Inspect the opening with the hole guide before filling anything.
Over-shared edges Use the focused topology repair workflow to determine the intended face.
Multiple vertex-connected components Review the separate-shell guide before keeping or removing any piece.

How it works: the server compares exact corner positions, skips zero-area triangles when counting edges, and groups pieces that share a corner. It does not check every corner for non-manifold problems or look for surfaces that cross each other. A damaged STL gives an error, never a clean result. If you only want to know about holes, use the STL Watertight Checker.
