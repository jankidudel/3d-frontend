Choose the measurement that answers your question. Overall bounds describe the occupied envelope; two selected mesh points describe a distance between those locations. Neither identifies an exact CAD hole centre or proves the dimensions of a printed part.

Measurement What it answers
Global axis-aligned bounds How far the current orientation spans X, Y and Z; not a named feature size
Two selected mesh points Direct distance between those surface locations and their axis differences
CAD dimension The intended analytic diameter, constraint or centre spacing
Physical measurement The produced part under the measuring method and conditions used

Rotation changes an envelope without stretching a part

The 40 × 20 × 10 mm control has a 40 mm long edge. Rotating it 45° around Z gives an expected global axis-aligned envelope of about 42.43 × 42.43 × 10 mm. Each planar span is (40 + 20) / √2. The longer envelope does not mean the part stretched. Compare the same orientation and coordinate system when two applications disagree.

Follow one measurement sequence

  1. Confirm unit and transform state. STL needs a supported assumption from a known dimension; a 3MF needs its unit and placed-item context. Record scale or rotation already applied.
  2. Read bounds in STL Dimensions or 3MF Dimensions. Compare the intended object versus the full arrangement.
  3. For approximate visible-feature checks, open STL Viewer or 3MF Viewer, activate Measure distance and pick two visible surface points. Read direct distance and axis differences.
  4. Compare the source CAD requirements. A mounting-hole centre distance, datum or exact cylinder needs its analytic definition rather than two surface picks.
  5. Record unresolved features and the next measurement method. A nominal bed comparison still needs actual slicer placement, supports, brim and exclusion checks.

Know what a mesh pick cannot establish

A faceted circle may not have vertices at opposite extrema. Viewing angle, selected facets and tessellation deviation affect a picked distance. Repeat the selection or inspect a section when useful, but do not relabel it as an analytic centre or a calibrated physical dimension.

For example, a 118 × 78 × 28 mm envelope fails a 120 × 80 × 25 mm requirement on height in that orientation. Even if another orientation fits, it says nothing about a required 64 mm hole-centre spacing. Keep those two decisions separate.

Pass physical questions to a physical record

Use the source design for nominal dimensions and the fit worksheet or axis worksheet for comparable produced-part readings and exclusions. State the instrument, method and conditions. A file check cannot certify the resulting assembly.
