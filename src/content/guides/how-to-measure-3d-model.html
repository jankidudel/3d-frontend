Choose the measurement that answers your question. Overall bounds describe the occupied envelope; two selected mesh points describe a distance between those locations. Neither identifies an exact CAD hole centre or proves the dimensions of a printed part.

Measurement What it answers
Global axis-aligned bounds How far the current orientation spans X, Y and Z; not a named feature size
Two selected mesh points Direct distance between those surface locations and their axis differences
CAD dimension The intended analytic diameter, constraint or centre spacing
Physical measurement The produced part under the measuring method and conditions used

Choose an angle or a three-point circle

Viewer mode Selected references and outcome
Angle Three mesh points A-B-C; B is the vertex. The unsigned interior angle is independent of camera perspective and uniform unit conversion. Follow the known-angle control.
Circle through 3 points A constructed radius and diameter in the plane of the three points. This does not detect a cylinder or prove roundness, minimum passage or printed fit. Use the faceted-bore and ellipse comparison.

For one reproducible sequence, load the angle coupon in the STL Viewer, choose Angle and Mesh vertex, select A=(10,0,0), B=(0,0,0), C=(5,8.66025447845459,0), then save a named record. The actual production calculation on reopened stored points gives about 60.0000012623 degrees. Orbit or reset the camera without changing the points. Download JSON to retain point order, selection basis and unit/frame notes. The manifest and observations distinguish stored mesh values from ideal arithmetic.

STL picks are restored to source coordinates after display centering. A 3MF pick uses resolved millimetre build coordinates and retains its placed-item ID. One source-coordinate unit becomes 1 mm under a millimetre interpretation or 25.4 mm under an inch interpretation. Displaying that latter value in inches gives 1 inch; conversion does not change the original interpretation. Unknown units leave physical length unavailable while valid angles remain available.

Identify the feature before naming a measurement

Use the existing measurement name and your retained notes for revision, feature role, selection basis, actual section/axis and unit assumption. The viewer does not recognise hardware features automatically.

Example feature-identification notes, not new viewer fields.
Named feature Record the construction and remaining question
Through-hole rim Selected rim points, section plane and bore axis; a constructed circle is not minimum passage.
Recess shoulder Selected shoulder span or profile points; source CAD defines bearing planes, analytic angle and seat contact.
Pocket flat Selected opposing surfaces and direction; source geometry and the assembly sequence still define corner clearance and full insertion access.

Save and download the existing local JSON measurement record before replacing the model. Selected surface spans do not verify analytic bores, seating contact or a collision-free insertion path.

Rotation changes an envelope without stretching a part

The 40 × 20 × 10 mm control has a 40 mm long edge. Rotating it 45° around Z gives an expected global axis-aligned envelope of about 42.43 × 42.43 × 10 mm. Each planar span is (40 + 20) / √2. The longer envelope does not mean the part stretched. Compare the same orientation and coordinate system when two applications disagree.

Follow one measurement sequence

  1. Confirm unit and transform state. STL needs a supported assumption from a known dimension; a 3MF needs its unit and placed-item context. Record scale or rotation already applied.
  2. Read bounds in STL Dimensions or 3MF Dimensions. Compare the intended object versus the full arrangement.
  3. For approximate visible-feature checks, open STL Viewer or 3MF Viewer, choose Distance, activate Start measuring and pick two visible surface points. Read direct distance and axis differences.
  4. Compare the source CAD requirements. A mounting-hole centre distance, datum or exact cylinder needs its analytic definition rather than two surface picks.
  5. Record unresolved features and the next measurement method. A nominal bed comparison still needs actual slicer placement, supports, brim and exclusion checks.

Know what a mesh pick cannot establish

A faceted circle may not have vertices at opposite extrema. Viewing angle, selected facets and tessellation deviation affect a picked distance. Repeat the selection or inspect a section when useful, but do not relabel it as an analytic centre or a calibrated physical dimension.

For example, a 118 × 78 × 28 mm envelope fails a 120 × 80 × 25 mm requirement on height in that orientation. Even if another orientation fits, it says nothing about a required 64 mm hole-centre spacing. Keep those two decisions separate.

Pass physical questions to a physical record

Use the source design for nominal dimensions and the fit worksheet or axis worksheet for comparable produced-part readings and exclusions. State the instrument, method and conditions. A file check cannot certify the resulting assembly.
