A degenerate triangle has zero area: two of its corners are in the same place, or all three lie on one straight line. It covers no surface, so removing it with a cleanup tool is usually safe. Very thin triangles that are not quite zero area need more care, because they may belong to a real detail.

The three cases

  • Repeated corner: corners at (0,0,0), (1,0,0) and (1,0,0). Two are the same point, so the area is zero.
  • Corners on a line: (0,0,0), (1,0,0) and (2,0,0). Three different points, but on one line, so the area is zero.
  • Very thin triangle: (0,0,0), (1,0,0) and (1,0.000001,0). Its area is tiny (0.0000005) but not zero. Some tools still treat it as degenerate.

Why the two checks disagree on the thin triangle

The browser preflight only counts a triangle as degenerate when its area is exactly zero. The server checker uses a relative rule: it flags a triangle whose area is extremely small compared with the length of its longest edge. So the very thin triangle counts as degenerate on the server but not in the browser. Neither result is wrong; they use different thresholds.

They also count pieces differently: the browser skips the zero-area face first, while the server counts the stray triangle as an extra piece. Neither count tells you which pieces you meant to keep.

Remove it, review it or re-export

What you find What to do Then check
A lone zero-area triangle Run cleanup on a copy. The full report, the size and the surface against the original.
Thin triangles on a real detail Don't delete them blindly. Re-export from CAD with different resolution settings if the detail matters. That detail in your slicer.
Zero-area triangles next to holes or overlaps Fix the hole or overlap in the CAD model. Deleting triangles alone does not rebuild missing surfaces.

Open a test file in STL preflight (runs in your browser), or use standard STL cleanup (uploads the file). Downloading a test file starts neither. After cleaning, check the saved file in the quality checker and your slicer. Removing degenerate triangles does not fill holes, fix flipped faces or prove the model will print.
