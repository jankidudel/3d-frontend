Choose STL for a geometry-only mesh handoff when the receiver accepts it. Choose OBJ when required appearance or grouping data is supported by that receiving workflow, and send every referenced companion file. Seeing the geometry open does not prove that its material or texture references worked.

What must survive?

Concern STL OBJ handoff
Surface representation Triangle facets This example uses polygon faces; the receiver may triangulate them
Physical size Agreed coordinate-unit convention Also needs an agreed unit convention
Appearance No standard texture/material data Material assignment can refer to MTL and image assets
Delivery inventory One geometry file All referenced files with stable relative paths

Richer appearance does not automatically improve geometric detail or printed colour. Ask whether the intended application uses the relevant properties and how they map to the actual manufacturing process.

Bundle anatomy: one original block

Download the complete owned OBJ bundle. It contains sample.obj, sample.mtl, textures/checker.png and README.md. The closed block spans X=0..20, Y=0..10 and Z=0..5 under the agreed millimetre convention. It has eight coordinate vertices and six outward-wound quad faces. The original blue/yellow checker image is 16 × 16 pixels. The assets are CC0-1.0 mathematical/appearance controls, not a captured import or printed specimen.

In sample.obj, mtllib sample.mtl names the library and usemtl checker assigns its material to faces. The MTL defines newmtl checker and map_Kd textures/checker.png. Each face references valid vertex and texture-coordinate indices. Renaming only the image breaks the relative image reference; omitting the MTL breaks the material-library lookup earlier.

Focused asset validation checks the inventory, safe reference paths, indices, UVs, closed outward edge incidence and analytical 1,000 mm³ material volume. These structural checks do not establish a particular application's rendered appearance. The asset inventory and hashes identify the distributed files.

Complete versus missing companion experiment

  1. Extract the complete ZIP into a fresh directory, retaining textures/checker.png at that relative path.
  2. In an application that supports OBJ/MTL/image textures, record its name, version and import options. Open sample.obj and inspect bounds, face assignments and texture orientation.
  3. Make a separate working copy and remove the texture. Record whether geometry loads, a warning appears, or appearance falls back.
  4. Restore the texture and remove the MTL instead. Record this different missing-reference stage without overwriting the complete bundle.
  5. Reopen the intact original and compare the actual required appearance.

Receiver appearance remains untested for this control. The experiment is for the reader to execute; the diagram is not a software observation. An illustrative swatch cannot prove import support or manufactured colour.

Record acceptance and choose the next stage

Record source/derivative revision, required grouping or appearance, unit convention, expected dimension, observed state and unresolved limitations in the receiving-file acceptance record. Leave unrun checks untested. Sending only sample.obj may still deliver geometry while failing the appearance requirement.

InspectMesh's tools in this workflow accept STL and supported 3MF, not OBJ, MTL, STEP or ZIP uploads. For a geometry-only derivative, export STL or supported 3MF from suitable authoring software, then inspect the saved mesh. An STL-to-3MF conversion cannot recover discarded textures. Preserve the appearance source when the job needs it.
