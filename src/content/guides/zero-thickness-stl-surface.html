A zero-thickness surface is a single sheet of triangles with no back side. It can look like a wall in a viewer, but it encloses no material, so a slicer has nothing to fill and usually skips it. The fix is to give the surface real thickness in your CAD program, not to change slicer settings.

Sheet vs thin plate

Download Shape Mesh check Volume
Flat sheet 20 x 20 x 0; 2 triangles 4 open edges, 1 piece Cannot be calculated: the sheet is open.
Upright sheet 20 x 0 x 20; 2 triangles 4 open edges, 1 piece Also open. Being tall does not give it thickness.
Closed plate 20 x 20 x 1; 12 triangles No open or non-manifold edges 400 cubic units.

STL stores no unit, so these are "mm" only if you open them as millimetres. The edge check does not measure wall thickness; we know the plate is 1 unit thick because we built it that way.

Too thin is a different problem

A closed but very thin feature can also go missing. Our T1 plaque has a letter O only 0.25 mm wide. It reopens as one closed 500-triangle shape, yet our saved PrusaSlicer 2.9.6 output has no paths for the O. That is a feature too thin for the profile, not a zero-thickness sheet. The embossed text guide compares both cases.

How to check your file

  1. Open it in the STL viewer, choose Inspect cross-section and move the cut through the surface. A sheet shows up as a line with no inside; a plate shows two faces with space between them. See the cross-section guide.
  2. Run it through the watertight checker. A sheet always has open edges around its border.
  3. Slice it and look at the first and middle layers. If the surface is missing, the slicer found nothing to fill. Some slicer modes can print a lone surface anyway, but that is a setting, not a fix to the model.

How to fix it

  • It should be a solid plate: give it thickness in CAD (or extrude it in a mesh editor), so it has a front, back and sides. Export a new copy and check it again.
  • It is closed but too thin for your nozzle: follow the wall thickness guide, then try your slicer's thin-wall settings.
  • The sheet is meant to be a sheet (for example a reference surface): leave it.

STL cleanup cannot create the missing back and sides of a sheet. If an edge really should be sealed, see fixing holes.
