To convert a 3MF to STL, open it in the 3MF to STL Converter, choose the whole build or one part, and export. The converter writes the shape in millimetres and checks the new file before you download it. Then open the STL as millimetres in your slicer and check one known dimension. Keep the original 3MF: the STL loses colours, materials, settings and the separation between parts.

Step 1: choose what to export and where it sits

In the converter, pick the whole build or one placed part. Copies placed more than once stay separate, and a part with nested sub-parts exports as one piece. By default the part keeps its position from the build plate; you can instead move it to the origin, and the report records how far it moved. Scale and rotation never change.

The check compares every triangle and reports the triangle count, size, position and any move applied. You can copy, save or print that report. It does not check for holes or overlaps, or test your slicer. If you are unsure whether to export one file or several, see combined or separate parts; if the result is the wrong size or in the wrong place, see STL from 3MF is the wrong size.

Step 2: check the result

Practise with three example files: one cube, the same cube placed twice, and an inch cube moved along X. Opened as millimetres, the results should be:

3MF STL you should get What it shows
One 20 mm cube 12 triangles; 20 mm cube The right build was selected
The same cube placed twice 24 triangles; 60 x 20 x 20 mm Both cubes are kept, but no longer as separate parts
A 1-inch cube moved 2 inches along X 12 triangles; 25.4 mm cube at X 50.8 to 76.2 mm Inches are converted to mm before the unitless STL is written

Our automated tests run the real converter on these files, save the STL and read it back to check sizes, triangle counts and positions. For your own file, open the STL in your slicer as millimetres and check a size you know.

What an STL keeps and loses

Information In the STL What to check
Shape (triangles) Kept Visible features and triangle count
Units and positions Converted to millimetres with positions applied; no unit stored Open as millimetres and check a known size
Separate parts Merged into one mesh (pieces may still be separate shells) That all intended parts and their layout are there
Colours, materials, settings Lost Keep the 3MF if you need them

A shape stored in the 3MF but never placed is not exported. For several separate STL files, export each part one at a time.

Worked example: the inch cube

Our inch example contains a cube 1 unit wide, declared in inches and moved 2 units along X. Read correctly, it is a 25.4 mm cube between 50.8 and 76.2 mm on X. Moving it does not change its 25.4 mm size.

  1. Open it in 3MF preflight and note its size.
  2. Convert it and save the STL under a new name.
  3. Open the STL in your slicer as millimetres.
  4. Check the 25.4 mm size, the position and the parts, then look at the sliced layers.

These values come from the example file and our software checks, not from a print. Some slicers re-centre or drop parts on import, so check the position as well as the size.

Before you send the STL

Compare Why
The part you selected vs the part exported Avoid sending the wrong part
Expected size vs the size of the reopened STL Catch unit or conversion mistakes
Expected position vs received position Tell a move apart from a resize
Information the recipient needs vs what STL keeps Make sure an STL is the right format to send

See also extracting one part from a 3MF, combined or separate parts and wrong size or position after export.

When not to convert

If parts are missing, the file uses features InspectMesh cannot read, or the recipient needs an editable project, go back to the program that made the file. Check missing objects after import before converting. Converting does not repair the mesh or rebuild missing parts.

The 3MF Core Specification defines objects, the build, units and positions (reviewed 7 October 2026). Check your slicer's support separately. Convert in your browser when a single shape-only file is what the recipient needs.
