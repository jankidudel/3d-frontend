A model can look perfect in a viewer while it has duplicate triangles, flipped faces or badly joined edges. Only a mesh check shows them. ---

To check an STL for errors, run it through a mesh checker such as the STL Quality Checker and look at each finding on its own. The common errors are holes (open edges), edges shared by too many faces, duplicate triangles and flipped faces, and each one needs a different fix. Before changing anything, decide whether the flagged area is a real mistake or part of the design, then fix one thing at a time and check the saved copy again.

Step 1: get a complete report

A file that failed to load, a cancelled check or a missing report does not mean the mesh is clean. For a quick check in your browser, use STL Print Preflight. For the full server check, use the STL Quality Checker (it uploads the file). Note which one you used and the unit you chose; the two can count separate pieces differently.

Step 2: learn the findings on example files

What the check shows What it means Next step
4 open edges on the 10-triangle example cube One side is missing; was it meant to be closed? Check the opening before filling it
1 duplicate; 3 non-manifold edges 13 triangles; one is an exact copy of another. Not the same as a flipped face Remove the duplicate
3 flipped-face conflicts, no open edges The surface is complete, but one triangle faces the wrong way Flip it back on a copy
2 pieces; no open or non-manifold edges Two closed parts, separate or overlapping (overlap is not checked) Decide whether both parts belong
12 of 36 wall samples below 1.2 mm Some walls look thin; samples do not cover every spot or measure strength Check those walls in the slicer
Unavailable volume or skipped fit Volume could not be calculated or no printer was chosen; neither means zero or passed Find out why volume is missing
  1. Load the closed cube from the example library and choose millimetres: 12 triangles, no open edges and 8,000 mm³.
  2. Swap in the cube with one side removed: 10 triangles, 4 open edges and no volume.
  3. Decide whether adding that side back matches the design. Fix a copy, reopen it and compare the area, the size and the sliced layers. A failed or empty report does not prove the fix worked.

If the file will not load at all, see STL file won't slice instead; repair tools cannot help until it opens.

One problem can show up twice

One extra copy of a triangle shows up as both a duplicate and as non-manifold edges. Separate closed pieces may be parts you meant to include. Look at the area and the design before treating a lower count as an improvement.

Step 3: choose one fix

Finding Ask first Next action
Open edges (holes) Should this surface be closed in the design? Check the holes
Non-manifold edge Is there an extra face, or do several surfaces meet here? Check the edges
Duplicate or zero-area triangle Is automatic cleanup safe for this file? Run cleanup
Separate piece Is it a real part or a stray fragment? Review pieces before removing any
Wrong size Which unit was the model designed in? Check size and units
File did not load, or no result What stopped the check? Go through the print-readiness checks

Step 4: check the fixed copy

Reopen the saved file itself, not the preview of the original. Compare the repaired area, the parts and the size, then run the same check again. Finally, look at the sliced layers with your real profile. A lower error count alone does not prove the shape survived.

When to return to the source model

Stop if you cannot tell from the mesh what a surface, joint or hollow should look like. Send the finding and its location to the designer instead of guessing. Use the print-readiness guide for the wider process decisions and the checklist to record unresolved work.
