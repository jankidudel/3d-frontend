Flipped normals are faces that point into the model instead of out of it. Before fixing anything, confirm they are really flipped: a dark patch in a viewer can just be lighting. If only a few faces disagree with their neighbours, a cleanup tool can turn them back. If the whole part is inside-out, flip the entire part.

Real flipped faces or just shading?

Each STL triangle stores its three corners in an order, and that order says which side is outside. Neighbouring triangles should run along their shared edge in opposite directions. InspectMesh checks that corner order, not the separate "normal" value stored in the file and not how a viewer shades it.

Two things this check cannot catch: a part that is completely inside-out (all faces agree with each other, just facing the wrong way), and a single sheet shown as a solid wall by a viewer that draws both sides.

Fix a few faces or the whole part

  • A small flipped patch on a closed part: run standard STL cleanup (it uploads the file). It fixes faces only in closed areas and reports what it changed.
  • The whole part inside-out: check the original design, then flip the entire part in your CAD or mesh editor.
  • The model also has holes, duplicate faces or overlaps: fix those first, using how to repair an STL. Face direction is unreliable until the mesh is closed.

Prusa's article on corrupted 3D models notes that PrusaSlicer repairs many models automatically and lists Meshmixer and Microsoft 3D Builder as free repair tools. Prusa documentation checked 28 November 2026.

Flipped faces or a mirrored part?

Flipping faces does not move a key, thread or text to the other side. If the whole part looks like a mirror image, see mirrored STL file. A mirrored part can pass all these checks.

Check the fixed copy

Save under a new name and run the quality check on the saved file. Compare triangle count, size, holes, non-manifold edges, flipped faces and the first sliced layers. Flipping faces does not fill holes or merge parts.
