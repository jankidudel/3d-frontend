G-code can go outside the print bed even when the print is small, because what matters is where the moves are, not just how wide the print is. A part can be placed off to one side, the file can assume a different bed origin, or travel moves at the start or end can leave the bed. Compare the lowest and highest X, Y and Z coordinates in the file with your bed's coordinates. A move outside the bed is a question to check for your printer, not proof of a crash.

Example: 10 mm wide but off the bed

In our example file, a travel move goes to X 230 and the next printing move ends at X 240. The printed line is only 10 mm wide, but on a bed from X 0 to 220 the whole line is off the bed. Comparing 10 mm with 220 mm would miss this. The file starts at X/Y/Z 0, in millimetres, with absolute positions. This is made-up geometry, not a real printer's usable area.

Printing moves vs travel moves

Our second example prints between X 10 and 110, Y 20 and 120, and Z 0.2 and 20, then travels to X 230. On a 220 x 220 x 200 mm bed, everything printed is on the bed, but the travel move goes off it. Note both. Prime or purge lines can also print outside the part's area, and travel includes start and end moves and Z hops.

Speed values don't move anything. Commands that reset the position or change modes do: see G92 and absolute vs relative mode before comparing with your machine.

How to check your file

  1. Open it in the G-code viewer and first check which commands it could read.
  2. Enter your bed's minimum and maximum X, Y and Z. A saved printer size alone does not tell you where the bed origin is.
  3. Look at printing moves, travel moves and any unsupported commands.
  4. Keep the result with your assumptions, then confirm the placement and start and end moves in your slicer.

G92 can change the reported position without moving the printer, so your firmware's documentation is needed to relate file coordinates to the real bed. InspectMesh flags G92 position resets as incomplete, and leaves G2/G3 arcs out of its size ranges.

Checking a model vs checking G-code
Check Based on Still to confirm
Model fit on a round bed The model's outline centred on the bed, plus height Where the model is placed and your printer's real usable area
G-code position The actual coordinates in the file, where they could be read Bed origin, offsets, printer limits and unread commands

A saved bed size cannot confirm the coordinates. Brims, skirts and supports can extend beyond the model. Never treat a partly read file as safe for your machine.
