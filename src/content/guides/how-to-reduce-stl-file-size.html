Identify what must become smaller: a transfer package, the STL's stored bytes or the triangle workload in the receiving application. Those need different operations. Preserve the original and accept a derivative only after the receiver opens it and the required features survive review.

Choose by the receiving constraint

Constraint Operation Verification
Only the transfer package is large Archive compression, with the STL extracted by the receiver The intended STL reopens after extraction; no geometry improvement claimed
Readable text has excessive storage overhead Binary encoding without intentional triangle removal Reopen records under stated Float32 storage and unit assumptions
Curves have unnecessary source detail Re-export from CAD with documented tessellation settings Check the named source feature and exported cross-section
The actual triangle workload is the problem Generate an independent simplified candidate Review changed surfaces, supported findings and receiver handling
The limit cannot be met without unacceptable loss Keep the original or use a suitable receiver/source workflow Do not approve an unverified feature to satisfy a byte target

Two measured controls

The owned ASCII three-piece control is 1,868 bytes. Its binary counterpart is 1,084 bytes. Both reparse to 20 triangles with the same coordinate bounds: X -8 to 31 units, Y 0 to 20 and Z 0 to 20. This fixture uses exact integer coordinates, so its Float32 binary values agree with the text values. Other ASCII decimals can round on binary export. Ordinary binary triangle records occupy 84 + 50N bytes for N triangles; 20 gives 1,084 bytes. Colour extensions or non-standard records need separate verification.

The 24-triangle raised-ridge coupon is 1,284 bytes. Its independently generated 75% target copy is 984 bytes and reopens at 18 triangles. Both retain 40 × 20 × 10.5 unit outer bounds, two shells and zero reported boundary or over-shared edges. A finite comparison at 5,000 surface samples in each direction and 0.1-unit tolerance measured a maximum of 0.2329025659 units, a mean of 0.00051183744 units and 25 samples above tolerance. These samples are not a whole-surface bound; equal outer bounds do not prove the ridge or a functional fit survived. The triangle-count guide owns the full ridge experiment and the rejected 50% alternative.

The ridge is a bounded simplifier experiment, not a generally suitable percentage prescription. The full candidate review retains requested versus actual counts and the new-findings decision. The encoding owner retains the numerical Float32 experiment.

Review the exact candidate in the receiver

  1. Record the receiving limit and the named feature that must remain.
  2. Generate the chosen operation in the existing reducer. Nothing simplifies merely on choosing a source.
  3. Retain the output filename and bytes. Reopen that exact derivative; compare supported topology findings and important geometry against the original.
  4. Inspect the critical feature and intended slicer layers or receiving operation. Record a successful reopen separately from feature acceptance.
  5. If a required feature cannot be verified, retain the original or return to source export. A smaller download alone is not sufficient evidence.
