A hole in an STL surface is an exposed triangle boundary, but a visible opening can also be an intentional passage. Check the boundary and the design before adding faces. InspectMesh can report open edges; its repair tool does not invent a missing surface.

Compare a known open and closed surface

Download the open-face cube and closed cube. Both use 20-unit sides, assumed to be millimetres here because STL stores no unit. The closed control has 12 triangles, zero boundary edges and screened volume of 8,000 mm³. Deleting one square face removes two triangles: the open control has 10 triangles and four boundary edges; its volume is unavailable, not zero. Run both through the watertight checker before interpreting your own file.

An open mouth on a thick-walled vessel can still have a closed material boundary: its inner and outer walls meet at a rim. A single uncapped sheet has exposed edges. Neither appearance alone tells you whether to cap the mouth. Compare the source design, nearby surfaces and intended channel before editing. If a designed channel is present in the source but fills in sliced layers, use the closed-channel slicer comparison rather than patching an opening.

Choose a repair from the intended surface

Finding Next action
A simple planar face is accidentally absent Patch a copy in a mesh editor if the plane and boundary are unambiguous; preserve the original.
A curved, threaded or mating face is missing Rebuild from source CAD or use reference geometry for a manual reconstruction. A flat automatic cap can alter function.
A long seam or several nearby boundary loops appear Check whether two borders need welding or a designed feature is missing. Do not cap each loop until the source shape is understood.
The opening is a designed channel or vessel mouth Keep it. Check whether the material walls form the intended closed boundary and inspect its sliced layers.
Separate fragments exist elsewhere Review them in STL Repair. Removing existing triangles does not fill the open cube's two missing triangles.

Verify the saved copy, then the slices

  1. Reopen the edited STL, not just the mesh-editor viewport. Compare the patched region, local silhouette, dimensions and face orientation with the original.
  2. Run the same boundary and broader preflight checks. Zero open edges confirms closure under that method, not the correct shape, absence of intersections or physical suitability.
  3. Inspect the affected layers in your selected slicer for missing or unexpected perimeters and a changed channel. Save the original and edited exports separately.

If the intended surface cannot be inferred, return to the design source. A visually closed or watertight mesh can still slice incorrectly or fail as a physical part.
