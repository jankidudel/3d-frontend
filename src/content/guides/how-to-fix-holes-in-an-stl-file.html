To fix a hole in an STL, first find it with a watertight check, then decide whether it is a real mistake or an intended opening. Small flat holes can be filled in a slicer or mesh editor; curved or functional surfaces should be rebuilt in CAD. Always work on a copy and check the sliced layers afterwards.

Step 1: find the holes

Run your file through the STL Watertight Checker. Any open edges mean the surface has a gap. To see what that looks like, download our open-face cube and closed cube. The closed 20 mm cube has 12 triangles, no open edges and a volume of 8,000 mm³. The open one is missing one side: 10 triangles, 4 open edges, and no volume can be calculated (that is not the same as zero).

Step 2: is it a mistake or part of the design?

Not every visible opening is a hole in the mesh. A thick-walled cup with an open top has inner and outer walls that meet at the rim, so the mesh is closed. A single sheet with no thickness does have open edges. Compare with the original design before filling anything. If a channel exists in the model but disappears when sliced, that is a different problem: see slicer fills a hole.

Step 3: choose the right fix

What you find Fix
A simple flat face is missing Fill it in a mesh editor or let the slicer repair it, working on a copy.
A curved, threaded or mating surface is missing Rebuild it in CAD. An automatic flat patch can change how the part fits or works.
A long crack or several small holes side by side Two edges may need joining rather than capping. Find out what shape was intended first.
The opening is meant to be there (channel, cup mouth) Keep it and check that the walls around it are closed.
Loose fragments elsewhere Review them in STL Repair. This removes pieces; it does not fill holes.

Fill holes with your slicer or a repair tool

InspectMesh finds holes but does not invent missing surfaces. According to Prusa's article on corrupted 3D models, PrusaSlicer repairs models automatically "to a certain degree", and on Windows 10 offers "Fix by the Netfabb" when you right-click the warning symbol next to the model name. For stubborn files, Prusa suggests Meshmixer ("Make solid" almost always works but can lose detail) or Microsoft 3D Builder on Windows. Prusa documentation checked 28 November 2026.

Step 4: check the repaired copy

  1. Reopen the saved STL itself, not just the editor view. Compare the patched area, the outline, the dimensions and face direction with the original.
  2. Run the watertight check again, plus STL preflight. Zero open edges means it is closed, not that the shape is correct.
  3. Slice it and look at the layers around the repair for missing or extra walls and blocked channels. Keep the original and the repaired copy.

If you cannot tell what the missing surface should look like, go back to the original design file.
