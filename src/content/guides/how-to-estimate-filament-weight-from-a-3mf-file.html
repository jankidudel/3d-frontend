A valid enclosed geometry volume and a chosen density give a solid-model-equivalent mass. That value is useful for comparing geometry or checking an implausible size; it is not the amount of filament your sliced job will consume.

Inputs: meaningful volume and a stated density

Use the 3MF volume checker to inspect the supported result and its limitations. Record resolved scope and physical unit before converting volume. A unit error is cubed in a volume calculation. Use a density documented for the actual formulation rather than assuming a material-family label supplies an exact value.

One volume-to-mass calculation

Mass in grams = volume in mm³ / 1,000 × density in g/cm³. A reported 32,000 mm³ is 32 cm³. With an explicitly assumed density of 1.24 g/cm³, the calculated solid-model-equivalent mass is 39.68 g. Use a density documented for your actual formulation when the result matters. The example is analytical arithmetic; 1.24 is an assumption, not an InspectMesh measurement or recommendation.

Show how a density assumption changes the answer

For the same 32 cm³ geometry, hypothetical densities of 1.20 and 1.30 g/cm³ give 38.4 and 41.6 g respectively. This is a sensitivity calculation, not a measured material range or an uncertainty interval. Record the chosen density, its source and the geometry scope beside the result so another person can reproduce it. Extra decimal places in the calculation do not establish the accuracy of either input. If the formulation's density is unknown, retain the volume and mark mass as unavailable rather than treating an assumed density as measured evidence.

Check validity and scope

Open surfaces, inconsistent orientation, overlapping shells or ambiguous geometry can make enclosed volume unavailable or unsuitable for a material sum. Unavailable volume is not zero. A multi-item 3MF is not automatically a valid sum of distinct solids; overlapping placements are not Boolean-unioned. Keep the original report and resolve ambiguity before treating the value as material.

Use local 3MF preflight to interpret supported geometry findings. Correct bounds or a visually plausible model do not by themselves validate enclosed volume, physical density or printability.

Move to sliced consumption for a production decision

Slice the intended arrangement with the actual profile and use complete per-material consumption for spool planning and production costing. Read support and purge inclusion before adding categories. Keep any reserve or financial uplift separate. A geometry mass cannot confirm that a spool is sufficient or establish a final customer quotation.
