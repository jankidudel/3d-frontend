A 3MF file can help you estimate how much material a print may use, but the result is an estimate—not a replacement for your slicer’s final filament calculation.

Use volume as a solid-model estimate

For a closed model, mass is approximately volume multiplied by material density. For example, a 20 cm3 solid volume at 1.24 g/cm3 is about 24.8 g before any slicer decisions. A sliced print can be substantially lighter or heavier because of infill, perimeters, supports, purge and process settings. Use a slicer's material estimate for purchasing or quoting a real print, and use this geometry estimate to compare designs or catch an implausible result. It cannot guarantee production-run consumption.

The geometry-to-mass formula

For a valid closed model, estimated solid mass equals resolved volume multiplied by material density. Keep units consistent: 1 cm³ equals 1,000 mm³. Convert a reported mm³ volume to cm³ before applying a density stated in g/cm³.

Worked density example

Suppose the resolved build volume is 32,000 mm³. That is 32 cm³. Using an illustrative density of 1.24 g/cm³ gives 32 × 1.24 = 39.68 g for a completely solid object. Use the density supplied for the actual material; formulation, fillers and manufacturer data can differ, so the example is not a material preset.

Why the sliced print differs

  • Infill replaces some solid interior with a chosen pattern and percentage.
  • Perimeters, top/bottom layers and thin-wall behavior change deposited volume.
  • Supports, brims, rafts, purge towers and tool changes add material.
  • Slicer flow settings and filament diameter affect the length estimate.

Use solid mass to compare model revisions or detect an implausible volume. Use the target slicer's material report for a real print and add only material absent from its complete report when purchasing or quoting.

Validity checks before calculating

Confirm declared units, resolved item count and a volume-compatible closed mesh. Open surfaces, self-intersections and overlapping build items can make volume misleading. For multiple parts, decide whether you need each object's mass or the entire resolved plate, and avoid double-counting overlapping placements.

Use the estimate for the right decision

Solid mass is useful for comparing two model revisions at the same assumed density or checking whether the package scale is plausible. It is unsuitable as a shipping, purchasing or customer-quote figure without sliced material, waste and measured process allowances. State the density source and calculation date so another person can reproduce the estimate.

The starting point is model volume. A larger solid volume generally needs more material, but actual filament use depends on the print settings you choose.

Calculate model volume

Upload the file to the 3MF Volume Calculator. It reports the volume of the resolved mesh geometry and can provide a material-weight estimate.

Choose the correct unit and material density. The same model volume weighs differently in PLA, PETG, ABS and filled filaments.

Understand the difference between volume and print weight

A volume calculator works from the model’s solid geometry. Your slicer may print that model partly hollow using infill.

Actual material use depends on:

  • infill percentage and pattern
  • wall/perimeter count
  • top and bottom layers
  • supports
  • brim or raft
  • purge material for multi-colour printing

Use the calculator for an early estimate and your slicer for the final print-planning figure.

Check mesh health first

Volume is most dependable when the model forms a closed, consistently oriented surface. Before relying on the result, use the 3MF Quality Checker and, where relevant, the 3MF Watertight Checker.

A model with holes or non-manifold geometry can produce an unreliable enclosed-volume calculation.

Use the estimate for planning

A rough weight estimate is useful when deciding whether you have enough filament, comparing design variants or estimating material cost before slicing.

Then open the model in the 3MF Viewer, confirm its dimensions and produce a final slice with your intended printer profile. The slicer remains the best source for the actual material estimate because it knows the print settings that will determine how much filament is deposited.

Replace geometry mass before a production job

After slicing the actual plate, preserve its complete per-material consumption, profile/version and repeat count. Do not transfer solid-model mass as a complete job input. The material reconciliation guide owns inclusion; the job calculator owns the selected production cost.
