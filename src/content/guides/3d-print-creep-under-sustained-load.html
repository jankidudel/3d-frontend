Read an illustrative time record and identify missing load and temperature evidence.' slug: 3d-print-creep-under-sustained-load eyebrow: Material evidence heading: 'Why can a 3D printed part deform under a steady load?' ---

Yes, a printed part can keep deforming under a constant load, even if it fit or deflected acceptably at first. Creep concerns deformation developing over time under stated loading conditions. Temperature, material condition, geometry and exact formulation matter. A room-temperature stiffness or tensile value is not a long-term deformation curve.

Separate four observations

Identify what is held and what is measured
Observation Controlled question Keep separate
Immediate loaded deformation How much does the measured geometry change when loading begins? The subsequent time-dependent increment
Creep How does deformation develop while the stated loading arrangement and conditions remain applied? Changes caused by temperature drift, contact movement or settlement
Recovery after unloading What displacement remains at each recorded recovery time? A permanent offset cannot be inferred from one early reading.
Stress relaxation How does stress or holding force change while deformation is constrained? Visible geometry alone does not measure force retention.

Instron's creep-test glossary describes continuous loading and separately explains relaxation under a given deformation. Those are distinct controlled questions. A hanging load, a clamped fastener joint, a snap held open and a locating feature need their own boundary conditions recorded; a real clamp can combine changing force, contact and displacement.

Read a time record from its unloaded reference

This is fictional arithmetic, not an InspectMesh test or a particular polymer's behaviour. Assume the same identified gauge references and measurement direction, a constant stated loading arrangement, stable temperature and material condition. The unloaded reference length is 50.00 mm. No instrument uncertainty or repeatability is supplied.

Hypothetical readings; nominal extension uses the 50.00 mm reference
State Gauge length Difference from reference Total nominal extension / reference
Immediately after loading 50.10 mm 0.10 mm 0.20%
After 1 hour under load 50.20 mm 0.20 mm 0.40%
After 24 hours under load 50.35 mm 0.35 mm 0.70%
10 minutes after unloading, illustrative recovery snapshot 50.04 mm 0.04 mm 0.08%

Each percentage is (reading - 50.00) / 50.00 × 100. The additional recorded extension from immediate loading to 24 hours is 50.35 - 50.10 = 0.25 mm. Calling all 0.35 mm time-dependent creep would include the immediate 0.10 mm deformation. The 0.04 mm residual is a snapshot at the stated recovery time; it does not establish completed recovery or permanence.

These geometry ratios are neither acceptance limits nor a creep law. They do not identify a material, quantify measurement uncertainty, predict rupture or justify extrapolating a straight line to a month or year. In an actual record, thermal change, gauge drift, support settlement and contact movement can also change displacement. Control those effects before assigning a cause.

Geometry changes the load-path question

Consider an ideal perpendicular 10 N force. At a 50 mm lever arm its moment is 10 × 50 = 500 N mm = 0.50 N m. At 20 mm it is 200 N mm = 0.20 N m. Moving a support or load therefore changes the mechanical question even with the same force.

This original arithmetic supplies no section stress, stiffness, creep, allowable load or bracket suitability. The root section, constraints, layer direction and contact geometry are still missing. A shorter arm is a design change to investigate, not a validated load rating.

Define the evidence a representative observation needs

  1. Identify grade/formulation, source revision, print revision, orientation, profile, finishing and initial material condition.
  2. Describe how the load is applied and supported, or which deformation is constrained. Record force if holding-force retention is the requirement.
  3. Record actual part temperature, exposure, elapsed loaded time and any changes. Separate unload time from recovery time.
  4. Use identified measurement points, direction and instrument; retain raw readings, repeats and relevant uncertainty instead of only an average.
  5. Agree the allowable geometry or force change and the relevant duration before interpreting the outcome. Twenty-four hours is not universally sufficient for service acceptance.

BASF's material-characterisation discussion identifies temperature, moisture, strain rate and direction as relevant inputs. Its modelling context includes other processing methods; moulded-polymer data and simulation capabilities do not become evidence for your FDM part. This guide performs no simulation or physical trial.

Choose a next action for the actual constraint

If a loaded holder moves, seek appropriate grade/time/temperature data and examine its load path. Keep the unresolved performance requirement visible when that evidence is absent. The material selector provides an editorial shortlist; initial success and a higher family band do not establish long-term capacity. The PLA and PETG profiles retain exact examples without recommending an automatic material upgrade.

For a constrained grip or snap, measure the separate holding-force question; it can lose force without an obvious position change. Hardness and HDT cannot provide a relaxation or compression-set value. Use the snap-fit guide for entry, engagement and reuse, and the fit worksheet for comparable dimensions. Critical application qualification needs appropriate engineering evidence beyond these planning tools.
