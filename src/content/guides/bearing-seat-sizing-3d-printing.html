Start with the exact bearing's outside diameter and width, then decide how the housing will locate and retain its outer ring. The bore through the bearing belongs to the shaft interface: an 8 mm bearing bore does not call for an 8 mm housing hole. Choose a trial fit for your process and verify a printed seat before making the complete part.

Identify three separate interfaces

Identify the contact before choosing a dimension.
Interface Dimension or decision Check separately
Outer ring and printed housing Bearing outside diameter and housing-seat diameter How this ring is located and retained in the application
Inner ring and shaft Bearing bore and shaft diameter Shaft location and its own retention arrangement
Axial shoulder or retainer Seat depth, ring-contact region and access Which ring it contacts, without rubbing a seal or casually clamping both rings together

The SKF 608-2RSH technical specification gives these nominal dimensions (checked 2 November 2026):

Manufacturer nominal dimensions for this exact designation, not measured sample values.
Bearing dimension Nominal value
Bore 8 mm
Outside diameter 22 mm

This regional manufacturer listing is a dimensional reference, not a stock, shipping or printed-fit recommendation. Identify your actual bearing before using these values. Its chamfer and abutment requirements need the current dimensional drawing; 8/22/7 alone cannot determine shoulder diameters.

Decide which ring should be held

A retained manufactured bearing ring has a different purpose from a freely moving printed joint. The manufacturer-hosted Shaft and Housing Fits reference distinguishes ring rotation relative to radial load and identifies material assumptions for its shaft and housing tables (printed pages 32-34, checked 2 November 2026). Fit depends on the load relationship and the housing/shaft material. This does not supply an FDM offset. Do not transfer metal-housing tolerance tables to a printed seat or assume that every rotating assembly needs the same outer-ring fit.

Work a nominal seat example

Illustrative arithmetic, not a tested bearing fit. Suppose the user selects a produced seat target of 22.20 mm around the nominal 22.00 mm outer ring, inside a concentric circular 30.00 mm housing.

Selected geometry and nominal gaps; all lengths are millimetres.
Quantity Calculation or input Basis
Bearing OD 22.00 mm Nominal SKF reference, not a measured sample
Trial produced seat 22.20 mm Hypothetical user-selected target
Diametral gap 22.20 - 22.00 = 0.20 mm Difference across the diameters
Ideal radial gap 0.20 / 2 = 0.10 mm Each side only when concentric
Housing outside diameter 30.00 mm Hypothetical circular geometry
Radial wall at target seat (30.00 - 22.20) / 2 = 3.90 mm Concentric annulus only

The 3.90 mm wall does not establish stiffness or holding ability. An offset bore, opening or screw slot can leave less material locally. A loose ring may need a separate retention strategy; this example does not select one. Use the tolerance calculator's Fixed external pin / hardware mode and its Fixed counterpart outside diameter (mm) field for the outer-ring OD. Desired clearance is diametral. The general fit guide explains the shared equations. This calculation does not select a bearing or verify retention.

Keep local correction separate from chosen clearance

In a separate hypothetical calibration, a 22.20 mm CAD test seat produces readings of 22.02, 22.04 and 22.06 mm. Mean = 22.04 mm; recorded range = 0.04 mm; mean error = 22.04 - 22.20 = -0.16 mm. These invented readings demonstrate arithmetic; InspectMesh did not measure a printed seat.

Under the same local-error assumption, targeting a produced 22.20 mm seat gives next CAD diameter = 22.20 - (-0.16) = 22.36 mm. With an unchanged concentric 30.00 mm outside, CAD radial wall becomes (30.00 - 22.36) / 2 = 3.82 mm. Recheck the actual edited housing. The recorded range is neither instrument uncertainty nor an acceptance interval.

Keep raw readings and conditions in the existing worksheet. Validate a new representative seat before transferring the correction to the full part. It does not automatically apply to another orientation, material, printer or axial depth. If a measured hardware OD replaces nominal 22.00 mm, recalculate against that input. Follow the local compensation workflow for the measurement procedure.

Check depth, shoulders and removal access

Seat depth is axial geometry, separate from radial clearance. A hypothetical straight recess 7.30 mm deep, measured from its entry reference plane to its shoulder, leaves 7.30 - 7.00 = 0.30 mm of simple depth allowance around the reference ring width. This is not a recommended end float: it says nothing about retainer position, shoulder compatibility, seals, preload or the shaft assembly. It is independent of the 0.20 mm diametral gap above.

Identify insertion direction, the intended ring-contact area and a practical removal route before closing the housing. Compare source shoulder and retainer geometry with the exact bearing drawing. A convenient entry chamfer cannot establish correct seating or compensate for an incompatible shoulder.

Inspect the exported seat at several levels

  1. Reopen the exact STL or 3MF revision and confirm its unit and placed scope.
  2. Inspect entrance, middle, shoulder and exit where present using visual cross-sections. A clip hides surfaces; it does not extract a measured contour.
  3. Record selected references with the hole-diameter construction workflow. Three points define a circle, not the minimum passage; facet flats or local entrance geometry may be more restrictive. A smooth shader is not an analytic bore.
  4. Inspect local walls, slots and nearby openings after every bore edit.
  5. Check the actual receiving-slicer paths and supports, including starts around the internal ring-contact region at the entrance, middle and shoulder. Moving a visible external seam does not establish an unaffected internal seat, retained ring or physical fit. Changing seat orientation needs new local checks rather than an unqualified reused offset.

Choose the next action from the observation

Observation Useful next action
Hardware dimensions unknown Identify the exact bearing and drawing.
Model seat uncertain Compare source dimensions, reopened mesh and several sections.
Physical seat too tight or cracks Locate entrance/first-layer interference, local size error and remaining material before choosing a correction.
Ring rocks or moves where it should be retained Revisit retention, local contact and the application requirements. Separate initial insertion from later seat-shape change and ring movement. Record temperature, load, duration and service condition; inspect movement under sustained loading before choosing another bore correction.
Unexplained rubbing or difficult removal Inspect axial contact, alignment and access separately from bore size.

A local test seat can investigate assembly under recorded conditions. Retain its revision and distinguish cracking, rocking, ring movement and rubbing. None of these questions is resolved by global scaling alone. Mesh checks supply no insertion-force, speed, load, lifetime or physical acceptance rating.
