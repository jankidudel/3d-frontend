Non-manifold means faces are connected wrongly: an edge has one face (a hole) or three or more. Self-intersecting means surfaces pass through each other in space. They are separate problems, and a mesh can pass a non-manifold check while its surfaces still cross. That is why "no non-manifold edges" does not mean the model is clean.

Four different problems

Problem Simple example What to look at
Hole (open edge) One side removed from a cube leaves 4 open edges Missing surface, or an intended opening?
Non-manifold edge A duplicated triangle makes 3 edges shared by 3 faces Duplicate or extra faces
Self-intersection Two closed cubes overlapping by 10 mm, each fine on its own Where surfaces cross; should they be merged or kept hollow?
Touching at one point Two parts meeting at a single corner Is there a real joint? Edge counts cannot tell.

An edge on a closed surface has exactly two faces; a hole edge has one; a non-manifold edge has more than two. Full manifold checks also look at corners (vertices), so passing an edge-only check is a narrower result.

STL preflight can look for crossing triangles in files up to its triangle limit. It reports an approximate location for each crossing pair, not an exact curve. Exactly overlapping or flat-on-flat faces are marked as unclear. Files that are too large or damaged are reported as skipped, never as clean. Removing duplicate faces does not fix surfaces that cross.

How to check and fix overlaps

  1. Run collection review or STL preflight and note the results and their limits.
  2. Look at the overlap in the STL viewer with a cross-section. It helps you see inside, but it is not a complete test.
  3. Check the CAD model: should the parts be merged into one solid, kept as separate moving parts, or does the overlap form a hollow?
  4. If they should be one solid, merge them (a Boolean union) in CAD or a mesh editor that has an intersection check.
  5. Export a new copy, compare it with the original and check the sliced layers in that area.

When not to merge

If merging would fill a hollow you need, or join parts that should move, ask the designer. A slicer that slices the model without complaint has made its own guess, which may not match the design.
