Use the print timeline and cooled measurements to choose the next check.' slug: 3d-print-warping-corners-lifting eyebrow: Print-stage distortion heading: 'Why are the corners of my 3D print lifting?' ---

Record when the lift starts. Loose lines during deposition call for a first-layer check. If a corner rises after a continuous base has printed, review the actual contact paths, footprint, cooling conditions, profile and build surface. If you only see the distortion after removing the part, record the release and how the cooled part sits before deciding what changed.

Uneven cooling and contraction restrained by the bed or adjoining material can contribute to distortion. Geometry, surface grip, airflow, the profile and the filament formulation can interact. A photo can identify the affected corner but leaves the timing and dominant cause open. Prusa's warping guidance illustrates lifted corners and why cooling choices can have different effects for different setups and features.

Find the earliest visible change

Checkpoints for distinguishing four similar-looking symptoms
When it appears Useful checkpoint Next check
As the first layer goes down Look for loose lines or missing contact from the start. Compare the saved paths to deposition and check the printer and sheet setup.
While printing, after the base was continuous Mark the first layer or elapsed stage when the base corner separates and where it sits on the plate. Compare contact at that corner with the generated paths, footprint and approved profile and operating environment.
First noticed after release Record whether you saw the base before release, how it was removed and how it sits after cooling. Measure against a consistent reference. Record the onset as unknown if the earlier state was unobserved.
At an upper unsupported edge Check whether the base is still seated and identify the first affected upper paths. Review the upper feature's orientation and support in the slice.

If a nozzle strike and a layer shift occur together, record what happened first if you observed it. The finished shape alone leaves that sequence open.

Choose one trial based on the affected corner

  1. Mark the corner or feature, its bed position and model orientation. Record whether it was attached at the first sign of distortion.
  2. Review the first-layer paths in the exact native slicer project. Check the maker's surface, profile and cooling guidance. Follow printer and filament requirements for the operating environment, enclosure, ventilation and equipment limits.
  3. Choose one supported change that addresses the observation, such as a brim, a different placement or a revised base. If you change orientation, also review supports, layer direction and the surfaces they affect.
  4. Inspect the generated outlines and clearance around the affected feature. Use the brim and support spacing guide for the clearance arithmetic. Keep access for removal and any specified separation layer in the plan. Grip and release both matter.
  5. Follow the maker's cooldown and removal procedure, then compare cooled readings with the same support points and references. Keep the initial and trial results separate before revising a production profile.

Worked example: corner gap and projected size

Take a hypothetical 80 × 40 mm base with the intended first-layer paths visible in the preview. In this example the first layer looks continuous, one corner separates during printing, and a gap remains when the cooled part is removed. The readings illustrate the method using invented data.

Three quantities from the hypothetical base
Quantity Reading Interpretation
Nominal X gauge span between two marked features 80.00 mm Design reference for the X comparison.
Cooled X span projected onto the reference direction at the same features 79.84 mm 80.00 − 79.84 = 0.16 mm shorter in projection, or 0.16 ÷ 80.00 × 100 = 0.20%.
Local gap at the marked corner on a checked reference surface 0.80 mm Out-of-plane reading at that corner with the part resting under its own weight.

For a repeat, identify the reference surface and supporting points, whether the part rocks, the measurement direction, temperature and instrument. For example, define X parallel to a marked edge on the reference, project the two gauge points to that direction and record how you measured the distance. Use a gap gauge at the marked corner without forcing the part flat. A new seating is a separate observation. A single corner reading describes only the gap at that point.

Dividing the corner gap by the nominal X span mixes an out-of-plane gap with an in-plane dimension. Even the 0.20% projected difference can include the effects of the distorted shape. Diagnose contact and distortion first. Set this part aside from the shrinkage calculator's measurement set until the seating and gauge method support a comparison. For assembly, use the fit measurement record to document how the mating pieces actually sit.

For material preparation, check the documented grade and sheet choices in the PC Blend profile or the ASA profile. A corner that changes after conditioning or under sustained service load belongs in a separate observation with its own time and conditions.
