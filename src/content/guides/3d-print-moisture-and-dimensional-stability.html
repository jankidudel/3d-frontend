Compare mass and length changes without inventing a swelling or scale factor.' slug: 3d-print-moisture-and-dimensional-stability eyebrow: Material evidence heading: "Can moisture change a 3D printed part's fit?" ---

Preparing filament for printing and evaluating a finished part after service exposure are different tasks. Record the state in which fit was measured. Moisture-related material changes may matter, but a mass-uptake percentage is not automatically the same percentage change in length, hole size or volume. Establish comparable conditions before choosing a dimensional correction.

Keep the stage and observation together

Four stages that a label such as “dry nylon” can hide
Stage Useful record Unresolved question
Spool preparation Exact filament, supplier drying instruction and recorded preparation Condition reached by a finished part in service
Storage and feeding Storage environment, exposure time and protection during printing Whether later part measurements share that state
Cooled printed part Cooling interval, temperature, axes and initial readings Equilibrium or long-term fit
Finished part after exposure Medium/humidity, temperature, duration, load and later readings Cause of any difference and whether it stabilises

Choose drying and storage equipment through the dryer versus dry-box guide. It owns filament preparation, not a drying recipe for an assembled part. A reference reading cannot be called dry or equilibrium merely because it was taken first.

Read absorption with its exposure method

The ASTM D570 public scope concerns water absorption under immersion. A humidity exposure or a supplier's internal procedure is not automatically that test. Keep medium, temperature, humidity where relevant, duration, specimen and initial state beside an uptake result. This guide does not reproduce the full procedure or claim compliance.

PA6, PA11, PA12 and reinforced grades are different formulations. The site's named nylon example is unfilled Prusament PA11. It has no specified dry-versus-conditioned mechanical protocol in the cited sheet. A PA6 or PA11-CF figure cannot supply the missing PA11 result, dimensional coefficient or service fit.

Two ratios describe different quantities

These are fictional readings from a hypothetical part. No physical test was performed and no claim is made that moisture caused every difference. The initial state is only a recorded reference, not an established dry or equilibrium condition.

Independent mass and selected-length arithmetic
Quantity Reference Later reading Relative change
Mass 20.00 g 20.40 g (20.40 - 20.00) / 20.00 × 100 = 2.00%
One selected length 50.00 mm 50.20 mm (50.20 - 50.00) / 50.00 × 100 = 0.40%

A 2% mass increase and a 0.4% length change are different observations. They supply no unmeasured axes, hole geometry, volume expansion or universal compensation percentage. Surface liquid, contamination, temperature and measurement method must be considered where relevant. Instrument resolution alone does not establish measurement accuracy or a causal moisture effect.

Do not enter the 2% mass value as dimensional shrinkage in the shrinkage calculator. Keep reference-condition and service-condition readings separate in its existing measurement/conditioning notes. A material-name change does not justify automatically applying a new scale factor.

A supplier example with explicit humidity

Page 2 of the historical Prusament PC Blend sheet, version 1.1, updated 16 February 2022, reports moisture absorption of 0.13% at 24 hours and 0.15% at seven days. Its method is labelled Prusa Polymers; footnote (2) states 24 °C; humidity 22%.

Those labels belong together. These are reported supplier observations for that blend, not generic PC properties, an immersion result or an ASTM D570 test. Neither row establishes saturation or a linear swelling model. They cannot turn the hypothetical length readings above into a manufacturer property or a scale correction. See the PC Blend profile for its separate loaded thermal evidence.

Check comparability before compensation

  1. Identify the exact grade, print revision, orientation and profile, then name each measurement state and its elapsed exposure.
  2. Retain the same axes, gauge references, feature definition and measuring method. Record instrument, temperature, cooling/conditioning and repeats.
  3. Specify the counterpart's state too. A sliding guide or bearing seat depends on both mating components, not only an outer length.
  4. Keep observations under different humidity, temperature, loading or time conditions in separate sets. Mixed states do not establish one stable printer correction.
  5. Define the representative service condition and acceptable fit change. Obtain exact-grade evidence or repeat a controlled comparison when it is missing; do not prescribe a universal conditioning time.

Use the existing fit worksheet for comparable dimensional readings. Use the shrinkage explanation when a repeatable process-size correction is actually supported. If the part changes while loaded, investigate time-dependent deformation and force retention rather than attributing every movement to moisture. For thermal numbers, read HDT versus Tg.

Sources reviewed 3 November 2026. The supplier observations and original arithmetic have different evidence bases; no uptake experiment, physical fit test or swelling simulation was performed for this guide.
