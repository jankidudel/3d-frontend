Heat deflection temperature (HDT) is a loaded deflection test result. Glass transition (Tg) describes a material transition measured by a specified analytical method. Neither number automatically gives the maximum operating temperature of your printed part. Start with the exact formulation and the test behind its number, then identify the part's temperature, load and required duration.

Name the temperature before comparing it

Five temperatures that answer different questions
Reported temperature Purpose What it leaves unresolved
Nozzle setting Processing the specified filament in a print profile Finished-part heat resistance
Bed setting Build-surface conditions during printing A temperature rating for an assembled part
HDT Deflection under the test's stated loading and heating conditions Long-term deformation in a different geometry or load
Tg A transition identified using a stated thermal-analysis method Melting temperature or a permissible service temperature
Documented service limit A separately supported application limit with its own scope Uses outside that load, duration, environment or part specification

The ISO 75-1:2020 public abstract describes deflection under flexural loading during a specified temperature rise. It cautions against using the result to predict end-use performance or endurance. This guide uses that public scope, not the purchased procedure, and describes no standards-compliant test performed by InspectMesh.

One PC Blend sheet, two test loads

Page 2 of the Prusament PC Blend technical datasheet, version 1.1, updated 16 February 2022, gives both rows below. This historical named blend is neither generic pure polycarbonate nor PC Blend Carbon Fiber. The PC profile's named example retains both observations with their loads.

Published Prusament PC Blend HDT results, same document
Test stress Reported HDT Method
0.45 MPa 113 °C ISO 75
1.80 MPa 93 °C ISO 75

The stated test stress changes by 1.80 / 0.45 = 4 times, while the reported temperatures differ by 113 - 93 = 20 °C. This describes these two rows. It provides no linear relationship, temperature allowance or interpolation for another bracket stress. Replacing 93 °C with 113 °C would erase the different loading condition.

HDT specimen preparation, orientation and conditioning are not specified for these rows. The sheet's separate printed-mechanical-specimen footnote must not silently fill those gaps. Keep missing fields visible when comparing another product. A higher number under a lower test stress does not establish better performance in your part.

Read Tg with its measurement basis

A DSC glass-transition result can use an onset, midpoint or endset criterion; heating rate and thermal history can change the reported observation. TA Instruments' thermal-analysis note explains these method distinctions. Comparing a midpoint from one protocol with an onset from another can confuse a method difference with a formulation difference.

Tg is not a melting point or an automatic use limit. The cited PC Blend sheet does not publish Tg, so a product Tg remains unavailable here. A different polycarbonate in an analytical-method example cannot supply it. Nozzle or bed settings cannot fill the missing field either.

Translate the application into evidence

For a motor-adjacent guard, distinguish ambient air from the actual part and contact temperatures. A sun-heated enclosure can also differ from surrounding air; no fixed offset applies. Define the location, range and duration of temperature exposure before choosing a datasheet comparison.

Requirements to resolve before accepting a heated part
Record Why it changes the question
Actual part temperature and duration A brief peak and sustained operation need different evidence.
Load, contact and support locations An unloaded cover and a fastened, loaded mount have different constraints.
Geometry, orientation and print revision Test specimens do not reproduce every wall, root or layer direction.
Environment and material condition Humidity, water or chemical exposure must match the intended use.
Allowable change and measurement method Acceptable movement or force loss is an application requirement, not a derived HDT margin.

Choose the next check

In the existing material comparison tool, retain property identity, units, method, test load, specimen, orientation, conditioning, heating protocol and formulation. Resolve missing or mismatched data with the supplier; matching fields improves source interpretation but does not approve the part. Request applicable grade data or obtain representative part-level validation rather than subtracting an arbitrary safety factor from HDT.

For shape change while loaded, continue to creep and force retention over time. For readings taken in different exposure states, use moisture and conditioned-fit interpretation. A short heating observation, an unloaded fit and long-term loaded service remain separate evidence.
