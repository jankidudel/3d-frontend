Choose source tessellation settings from the curve you need to approximate, then inspect the actual exported facets. Chord deviation, angular constraints and triangle count describe mesh geometry; none certifies a printed dimension.

Apply sagitta to an internal polygonal bore

For an ideal regular n-sided opening with vertex circumradius R=10, the flat span is 2R cos(π/n). The deficit from the 20-unit vertex circle is twice the sagitta. The original 12-sided and 48-sided closed bore controls have identical 30 by 30 by 8 outer bounds.

These are analytical ideal-section values; stored Float32 coordinates introduce small differences. Actual three-point constructions on the reopened cardinal vertices return about 20 for both bores. The viewer does not calculate an inscribed passage. The hole diameter guide owns selection and interpretation, including an ellipse counterexample; this page retains the tessellation mathematics.

A nominal circle or across-vertices result can exceed the limiting flat span. Before inferring hardware passage, compare the actual source approximation and relevant counterpart envelope. The 12/48-sided bore controls above retain their circumradius convention; no printed hardware clearance is established by their analytic values.

What each setting means

A polygonal curve already present in the mesh is a source-approximation question. Stair stepping through height is a layer-sampling question. Finer layers do not restore omitted tessellated shape, and finer source facets do not change the print schedule.

Chord height/deviation is a length between the source curve and its straight approximation. Angular tolerance limits a change of direction. Other constraints, such as minimum facet width, can alter the result. A total triangle count does not specify where detail is distributed. Keep the exporter's units and parameter meanings explicit.

A circular cross-section gives a checkable example

For a circle of radius R, replacing an arc with a straight chord creates a maximum inward gap, the sagitta s. If the arc spans angle θ, s = R(1 - cos(θ/2)). For N equal segments around a circle, θ = 2π/N and s = R(1 - cos(π/N)). This is a two-dimensional mathematical example, not the complete rule of a CAD surface mesher.

Radius Segments around circle Angle per segment Maximum sagitta
10 mm 24 15° 0.08555 mm
10 mm 72 5° 0.00952 mm

The same angle on a larger radius creates a larger absolute gap. To aim for s ≤ 0.01 mm on this 10 mm circle, N must be at least π / arccos(1 - 0.01/10), approximately 70.24; round up to 71 equal segments. A 72-segment example meets that idealized cross-section condition. It does not establish a 0.01 mm tolerance for an arbitrary three-dimensional export or a printed part.

  1. Download the 24-segment control and the 72-segment control. Their designed circumradius is 10 mm and height is 20 mm; a vertex lies on +X.
  2. Open each in STL Viewer, assume millimetres and look down Z. Compare the silhouette at the same scale. Cross-section display can help inspect the intermediate facets; mesh point picks do not recover an analytic cylinder.
  3. Review the actual serialized/reopened observations. Independent edge-incidence, winding, bounds and count checks ran locally. Sagitta remains an ideal-circle calculation, not a measured exporter setting or physical result.
  4. When exporting your own CAD, retain its radius, exact settings and application version separately. These files were generated mathematically, not by Fusion or Onshape.

Why the settings remain separate

Chord deviation controls geometric departure; angular tolerance controls direction change and can force finer segmentation even where an absolute gap looks small. Exporters may also impose edge-length, minimum-facet or other meshing constraints. Units and exact parameter meanings must come from the particular exporter. Do not enter degrees into a field that expects radians or a length into an angle field.

The current Onshape export documentation lists separate custom STL chordal tolerance, angular deviation and minimum facet width. Autodesk's mesh export help describes surface deviation and refinement controls. Reviewed 26 October 2026; installed CAD builds were not run. Check the labels and units in your exact version.

Stop when the required source approximation cannot be verified

No universal chord tolerance or angular setting suits every shape. Return to the analytic source when an interface depends on a cylinder or datum that the mesh cannot establish. Count interpretation explains complexity; revision comparison separates finite surface samples from named-feature checks. Later simplification acts on triangles and does not inherit the original CAD tessellation condition.
