Resolve customer intent and physical dimensions before deciding whether to accept a print file. Keep mesh findings, manufacturing suitability and the price quotation as separate decisions. A clean topology screen cannot establish material performance or an acceptable functional fit.

Intake form example

Customer/project reference: optional local label
Files and revision: bracket-r04.stl, cap-r02.3mf
Quantity: one bracket and two caps
Known dimension: bracket mounting centres 40 mm
STL assumption: millimetres; confirm with customer
Process/printer: customer requirement or proposed machine
Material/finish: exact requested grade and colour, or clarify
Functional surfaces: mounting holes and mating cap face
Use conditions: indoor/outdoor, load, temperature - clarify
Delivery: required date and permitted design changes
Approval needed: dimension, orientation and quote assumptions

This is a hypothetical intake record. Do not infer the load or tolerance from the filename. Ask for a drawing or reference dimension when the use is functional. Record whether you are providing file review only, a prototype or a finished part with agreed requirements.

Accept, clarify or return to source CAD

Observed condition Disposition Next action
Known dimensions agree; completed checks understood; intended parts present Accept for slicing review Confirm profile, supports and quote assumptions; acceptance is not print certification
STL scale unknown, extra shells or missing process requirements Clarify Ask for a known dimension, intended parts and material/process requirements before changing anything
Malformed input, missing functional surface or ambiguous overlapping solids Return to CAD Request a corrected source export and retain the failed report
Nominal fit passes but brim/support footprint exceeds usable bed Revise manufacturing proposal Discuss orientation, splitting or another machine; do not silently shrink a functional part

Review a collection without hiding one failed file

Run the example collection before inspecting customer files. The 20 mm closed cube completes; the open-face cube has four boundary edges and unavailable volume; malformed text fails independently. Use each file card to rerun or replace a corrected export, then export all results with an optional project reference. Original files stay in tab memory unless you explicitly choose a tool that uploads.

Use the per-file handoff to STL preflight or 3MF preflight. Review destination settings separately. Reloading clears the browser-local file context and requires reselection. Report history holds filenames and findings only, not original geometry or cloud backups.

Keep the quote independent of mesh health

A model with no completed-check findings can still need expensive supports, repeated setup, finishing or a process you do not provide. A topology warning may represent an intentional open surface. Explain the observed condition and ask the customer rather than treating all warnings as rejection.

Hypothetical customer response: “The bracket is 80 mm wide under the supplied millimetre assumption. One open boundary remains at the mounting face; please provide a corrected source export. Printer fit and the quotation are pending.” This gives an actionable decision without claiming the software knows the intended repair.

Once the intake questions are resolved, use the quote workflow and the price calculator. Keep the agreed material, quantity, revision and finishing scope with the review report. For changed geometry, compare the original and revised STL before approval.

Prepare a local quote worksheet

After checking the intended files and scope, use the price calculator and printable quote worksheet to record quantity, currency, per-part work and once-per-order setup. Keep failure allowance separate from rates that already include it. The worksheet is an estimate, not an invoice or production acceptance; references stay local until deliberately exported.
