--- title: 'Will a Model Fit a Round 3D Printer Bed? | InspectMesh' description: 'Check a model envelope against a circular usable region. Understand corner clearance, centred placement and why separate X/Y spans can give a misleading fit.' slug: round-3d-printer-bed-fit eyebrow: Build space and placement heading: Will Your Model Fit a Round 3D Printer Bed? ---

A model can be narrower than the bed diameter in both X and Y and still put its corners outside a round bed. Check the complete envelope against the circle, then review actual placement and generated structures in the intended slicer.

Start with the usable region

Record a documented usable diameter D and usable Z height. If the diameter already excludes an edge band, do not remove that same band again. An additional radial edge exclusion e leaves radius R = D/2 - e. These inputs describe an ideal disc at every height up to Z. They do not establish height-dependent reach, clips, probe exclusions or complete machine kinematics.

Choose Circle explicitly in the 3D Printer Fit Checker. Equal X and Y entries never select a circle automatically. The named-printer choices retain their documented nominal box basis; use a custom circle only when you can supply its usable dimensions. Confirm legacy saved Width/Height/Depth values as X/Y/Z before reusing them. The tool preserves old stored values rather than guessing which meant vertical height.

Compare the diagonal, not just X and Y

For a centred rectangular envelope W wide along bed X and H along bed Y, the farthest corner is r = hypot(W/2, H/2) from the centre. The envelope is contained when hypot(W,H) <= D - 2e. The separate model Z extent must also be no greater than usable Z. This calculation encloses the full XY projection, not just the first layer touching the bed.

Diameter XY envelope Radial exclusion Observation
220 150 x 150 0 Diagonal 212.132; contained, with radius slack about 3.934.
220 150 x 150 5 Effective diameter 210; envelope not contained, though each span is below 210.
220 160 x 160 0 Diagonal 226.274; envelope not contained.
220 180 x 20 0 Diagonal 181.108; centred envelope contained.

Radius slack R-r measures the closest ideal boundary distance at the farthest corner. It is neither separate X/Y clearance nor a manufacturing tolerance. The implementation compares stored numbers with a strict inclusive boundary, without a hidden epsilon or physical allowance. Exact tangency leaves no intentional reserve; rounded displayed values must not decide a boundary case.

Understand what rotation can change

A rigid rectangle rotated in its own plane around the disc centre keeps the same corner radius. Swapping 150 x 150, or turning a 180 x 20 rectangle to 20 x 180, cannot repair an excessive diagonal. Choosing a different source axis as build Z changes the XY envelope and height, so a different right-angle candidate can work. The orientation planner compares six build directions, including their downward area and contact estimates; containment alone does not select the best printing orientation.

An irregular model rotated and measured again can have different axis-aligned bounds. That is a recomputed geometry question, not evidence that rotating the original bounding rectangle shrank its diagonal. InspectMesh's fit comparison neither searches all angles nor exports a rotated file.

Check position separately

A 60 x 60 rectangle centred at planner offset (70,0) from a circle centre (0,0) has its farthest corner at (100,30). Its radius is about 104.403 mm, inside R=110. Shift the centre to (80,0) and that corner becomes (110,30), about 114.018 mm away: outside. Identical spans can therefore produce different placement observations.

The bed layout planner checks all four corners at each suggested position. Its diagram uses an internal planning frame; those coordinates are not inferred machine coordinates or the source build origin. The centred fit checker assumes a hypothetical centring operation and does not perform it. Diagnose actual source minima/maxima with the off-bed placement workflow.

Read conservative rejection correctly

Envelope contained means the geometry enclosed by that rectangle is also inside the ideal disc at the stated placement. Envelope not contained says the rectangle is outside. Empty corners can make this conservative: an analytical cross made from a 180 x 20 horizontal bar and a 20 x 180 vertical bar has every outer corner within about 90.554 mm of its centre. Its 180 x 180 enclosing box has corner radius about 127.279 mm. On a radius-110 disc the cross is inside while the box fails. This is a mathematical counterexample, not a downloaded model, inspected customer file or printed test.

Reproduce a tool observation with an existing control

Download the existing 250 x 210 x 180 STL box. In the fit checker, retain the millimetre STL assumption, select Current orientation only, enable custom/session profile, choose Circle, diameter 330 and usable Z 200. Leave edge exclusion and extra allowance at zero. The source XY diagonal is about 326.497 mm, so the centred envelope is contained with radius slack about 1.752 mm and Z slack 20 mm. Set radial edge exclusion to 2 mm: effective diameter becomes 326 and the envelope is not contained. These are local software observations using original geometry, not a native slicer or physical result.

Carry the basis into the sliced job

Keep model scale, fixed physical allowances and placement distinct. With zero extra expansion, the maximum uniform envelope factor is the smaller of (D-2e)/hypot(W,H) and usableZ/modelZ. If an extra per-side allowance a is entered, the tool instead solves (sW+2a)^2+(sH+2a)^2 <= (2R)^2: a stays in physical millimetres. The suggested scale is capped at 100%, leaves the original untouched and is not a hardware-fit recommendation.

Finish with the actual native placement and space added by brims, skirts and supports. A decorative bed image is not verification of a printable region: Prusa's custom bed model documentation describes a visual STL and its origin, not certification of your machine's usable shape (reviewed 1 November 2026). Keep the exact project/profile for the final machine checks.
