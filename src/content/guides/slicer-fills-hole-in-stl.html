When a slicer fills a hole or channel you designed, there are three usual causes: the model has a hidden cap over the opening, another part overlaps the opening, or the gap is too narrow for your nozzle and line width. Check the model first, then the slicer settings. A watertight result does not prove the hole is there: a solid block is watertight too.

This is a different problem from a missing roof or top layer. If the top of your part is not printing, see missing top layers. Changing surface options such as ironing will not bring back a filled hole.

The open-channel G-code keeps the channel clear on its first and middle layers. The solid and overlapping files produced identical G-code, even though the models are built differently. Only the slicer preview shows the difference; an open-edge count cannot.

How to find and fix the cause

Our test settings: PrusaSlicer 2.9.6, default 200 x 200 mm bed, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.45 mm line width, 0.2 mm layers, 2 perimeters, 100% rectilinear infill. Software results only; nothing was printed.

  1. Look for a cap. Open the original in the STL viewer. If the opening is covered in the model itself, fix it in CAD. (A missing surface is the opposite problem: see fixing holes.)
  2. Look for overlapping parts. Run STL preflight and check the number of pieces. If another solid overlaps the channel, subtract or re-merge them properly in CAD.
  3. Check the gap size. If the model is fine, find the layer where the channel disappears and compare its width with your nozzle and line width. Widen it in the design or try a reviewed slicer setting rather than scaling the whole part. For letters and engraved grooves, see embossed text not printing.
  4. Recheck. Slice the fixed file and step through every layer of the channel, not just one.

Thin walls are covered in the wall thickness guide. Slicer paths do not prove the printed hole will be the exact size.
