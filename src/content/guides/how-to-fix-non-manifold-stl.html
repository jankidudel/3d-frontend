A non-manifold STL has edges that do not join exactly two faces: usually a hole (an edge with one face) or extra faces meeting at one edge. Find which one you have with a mesh check, fix that one problem on a copy of the file, then check the copy and its sliced layers again. Separate parts and overlapping solids can trigger similar slicer warnings, but they are not non-manifold faults and need a different decision.

Step 1: find out which problem you have

Run the file through the STL Quality Checker and match the result:

What the check shows What it means Fix Stop if
Open edges The surface has a hole Fill or rebuild the missing faces. See fixing holes. The opening is meant to be there, or you cannot tell its shape.
Duplicate faces and non-manifold edges A triangle is stored twice Remove the copy with STL Repair. The "duplicate" actually belongs to another part.
Several pieces, no edge problems Separate parts in one file Keep them, or export them separately. See multiple shells. Merging would remove a needed gap.
No edge problems, but parts visibly overlap Solids pass through each other Merge them in CAD only if they should be one solid. See overlapping surfaces. Only the designer knows the intended shape.

The edge check compares exact corner positions; it does not test whether surfaces cross. Parts touching at a single corner also need a visual check: InspectMesh's server report may count them as one piece, while the repair tool's part selector treats them as two. The CGAL manual likewise keeps edge checks and overlap checks separate.

Step 2: practise on three example files

  1. Hole: download the open-face cube and the closed cube. The open one is missing one square side (two triangles). Adding those two triangles back gives 12 faces and no open edges. Filling an unknown opening automatically could instead seal a hole that was meant to be there.
  2. Duplicate face: download the 13-face cube. Standard STL Repair removes the copied triangle and saves a 12-face cube of the same 20 x 20 x 20 size with no non-manifold edges. The same action can also remove zero-area faces and fix some flipped faces, so read its full report.
  3. Overlap: download the crossing cubes. The report shows 24 faces and no open or non-manifold edges, because the overlap check is not part of it. Merge them in CAD only if they are meant to be one object.

Step 3: try your slicer or a repair tool

Prusa's article on corrupted 3D models says PrusaSlicer repairs models automatically "to a certain degree". On Windows 10, you can right-click the warning symbol next to the model name, or the model itself, and choose "Fix by the Netfabb". Prusa also suggests dedicated tools such as Meshmixer, whose "Make solid" option "almost always works" but can lose detail, and Microsoft 3D Builder on Windows. Prusa documentation checked 28 November 2026; repair services change, so check that the option exists in your version.

Whatever tool you use, compare the result with your design. Automatic repair can close an opening you wanted or merge parts that should move separately.

When not to merge parts

Two parts touching at a point or an edge do not make a strong joint. A print-in-place hinge or two separate parts need their gap. An inner wall may be a hollow you want to keep. A blanket "merge all" can destroy any of these while the edge counts look clean. For moving parts, see print-in-place clearance: a part that only binds when moving is usually a slicer or design issue, not a mesh fault.

Step 4: check the repaired copy

Save the repaired STL under a new name, reopen it and compare: triangle count, overall size, open and non-manifold edges, flipped faces, intended holes, and the first sliced layers around the repair. If fixing it means guessing the shape of a missing or load-bearing area, go back to the CAD model instead.
