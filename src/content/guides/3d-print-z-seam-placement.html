A perimeter loop has a start and an end, and their transition can leave a visible mark. Choose a location that respects both visible and mating faces, then verify the generated slice. Moving a start changes process placement; it does not repair or resize the source mesh.

Identify the mark before moving it

Compare three stages: the source surface, the native slicer's start/transition markers and the physical sample if one exists. A ridge already present in CAD, an open mesh boundary, support contact and inconsistent extrusion need different investigations. An STL triangle edge does not automatically locate a perimeter start. This Z-seam question also differs from the joining seam between separately printed sections.

Inspect the source in the STL Viewer and use mesh diagnosis for an actual source fault. Bounds, volume and triangle count cannot identify how a slicer chooses loop starts.

Map visible faces and protected interfaces

Less-visible region B Check whether it is also a contact, clip or locating surface. A rear-looking face is not automatically functionally unimportant.
Bore or bearing seat Name the internal contact region, entrance and depth. Inspect its own starts; moving an external seam does not establish internal clearance or retention.
Mating lip or moving gap Protect the actual contacting and swept regions. Review native paths and, where required, a representative physical interface.

Keep orientation a compromise among contact, supports, fit and feature needs. Use the orientation workflow first if the face changes direction. An envelope-fit candidate does not choose its best-looking seam.

Compare placement strategies

Prusa's seam-position reference documents controls including Aligned and Random. Grouping starts concentrates their locations; distributing them spreads the transitions. Neither strategy establishes an invisible mark or a dimensionally better part. Compare where the generated starts actually land rather than selecting a label alone.

Prusa's seam-painting reference describes selecting allowed or blocked regions in the native application. InspectMesh does not edit those selections or retain slicer painting. Review the interaction with placement settings in your application/version, then reslice the intended source.

The seam-position reference also describes scarf transitions: a version-specific transition option, distinct from merely relocating a start. Check availability and effective settings in your own application. This guide does not claim that option was used in InspectMesh's retained PrusaSlicer 2.9.6 examples or establish a strength or visibility outcome.

One contour with two different starts

Consider an original mathematical 30 × 30 mm square centreline in the XY plane at one selected layer. Each listed pair is (X,Y) in millimetres. This is neither a deposited-width boundary nor exported slicer output. Both contours traverse the same direction and close at their respective starts.

  1. A: (0,0) → (30,0) → (30,30) → (0,30) → (0,0).
  2. B: (15,0) → (30,0) → (30,30) → (0,30) → (0,0) → (15,0).

A has four 30 mm sides. B splits the bottom side, giving 15 + 30 + 30 + 30 + 15 = 120 mm. Both enclose 30 × 30 = 900 mm². A corner start and a mid-face start can therefore trace the same geometry. Identical source shape and its mesh properties do not fix the start location.

This calculation simulates no candidate-selection algorithm, deposition, retraction, pressure transition, total travel or duration. It assigns no physical protrusion. Multiple islands, perimeters and transition methods mean an actual job need not have one seam per layer; travel Z changes do not supply a layer count.

Inspect the native slice through the affected heights

  1. Retain the exact source revision, rotation, slicer/version and effective profile, including local overrides or painting.
  2. Use native markers/categories to inspect external and internal perimeter transitions around the named face and interface.
  3. Step through openings, contour changes and ledges, rather than approving the whole part from one screenshot.
  4. Recheck after rotation, profile or placement changes and identify the exact saved output.
  5. Compare a representative physical sample when appearance or fit requires one; record its viewing or measuring direction and outcome.

The bounded G-code viewer recognises supported motion and state, not a complete seam or ironing legend. Every endpoint, retraction or Z move is not automatically a seam. Use the native slice for process intent and keep unsupported coverage visible.

Separate location from transition quality

If placement is stable but blobs vary, return to the relevant delivery and pressure/retraction evidence through calibration and feed versus profile flow. Do not prescribe a new firmware value just to move a mark. A different continuous spiral process has feature restrictions; it is not a general substitute when a part needs ordinary walls, infill, separate regions or a roof.

For a bearing housing or moving joint, review the actual interface separately. A three-point mesh circle does not measure deposited protrusion, prove release or certify a retained ring.
