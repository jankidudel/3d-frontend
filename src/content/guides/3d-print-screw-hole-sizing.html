Choose what the hole must do before choosing its diameter. A clearance hole lets the screw pass; a pilot supports a particular thread-forming or cutting operation; an insert seat follows the selected insert drawing. The same screw label does not give these holes the same dimensions.

Choose the hole's role

Start with the receiving feature, then obtain its dimensional basis.
Intended action Dimension basis Verify separately
Screw passes to a nut or threaded counterpart Relevant maximum passing envelope plus chosen passage and positional allowance Shank/thread passage, head seat, alignment and driver access
A specified screw forms a thread in plastic Exact screw-family, material and application guidance Pilot, boss, usable depth, assembly method and process suitability
Hole is tapped after printing Exact thread, tap and preparation process Preparation diameter, usable depth, tap runout/access and finished thread
Thread is modelled and printed Defined thread form and pitch for the intended process Exported detail, actual sliced paths and physical counterpart engagement
An insert is installed Exact insert series and length drawing Seat, depth, boss, installation and service requirements

A clearance passage is not intended to grip the screw's thread. Conversely, increasing a thread-forming pilot until the screw falls through defeats that receiving feature. For example, EJOT's DELTA PT documentation ties its direct-fastening design support to the particular screw, material and pilot geometry (reviewed 1 November 2026). It does not supply a universal FDM pilot prescription. A wood- or metal-drilling chart is not automatically a thermoplastic printing specification either.

Identify the actual screw and receiver

Record the thread system and pitch, exact part or drawing revision, head style, washer if used and receiving nut, insert or plastic thread. Establish which portion must pass through each plate: thread crests, a plain shank or a larger shoulder may set the envelope. Keep nominal catalogue dimensions, their tolerances and your actual hardware measurements in separate fields.

A single mean reading does not establish the maximum envelope of a hardware batch. Likewise, a diameter allowance cannot by itself resolve misaligned hole axes or mounting-centre errors. Record the intended alignment and inspect it in source CAD before treating the problem as local hole compensation.

Work a clearance-passage example

The following inputs are hypothetical arithmetic, not measured InspectMesh hardware or recommendations for every nominal M4 screw. Assume the relevant external passing envelope is 3.95 mm and choose a desired produced diametral clearance of 0.35 mm.

  1. Produced passage target: H = 3.95 + 0.35 = 4.30 mm.
  2. Assume comparable local hole error eH = produced - designed = -0.15 mm.
  3. Provisional source test diameter: H - eH = 4.30 - (-0.15) = 4.45 mm.
  4. For ideal concentric circles only, the chosen one-side gap is 0.35 / 2 = 0.175 mm.

The chosen 0.35 mm gap and assumed -0.15 mm process error serve different purposes. Enter the 3.95 mm external envelope and 0.35 mm clearance manually in the tolerance worksheet using Fixed external pin / hardware. Retain the source test size and comparable produced readings for the hole-error calculation. Do not enter an outside printing correction for hardware that is not being printed. A local test must establish whether the assumed error transfers to this feature.

If the opening is irregular or tight only near one end, use the bore-orientation diagnosis before applying a scalar change. For a stable local difference, follow hole compensation; avoid applying the same baked-in correction again in the slicer.

Check screw reach separately from passage

For a hypothetical screw whose drawing defines 12.0 mm from the under-head bearing plane to its tip, a bearing-seat-to-interface stack of 8.5 mm leaves 12.0 - 8.5 = 3.5 mm projection beyond that interface. If a blind receiver has 5.0 mm available geometric depth, the corresponding end allowance is 5.0 - 3.5 = 1.5 mm.

Neither number establishes usable threaded engagement. The screw tip, thread runout, incomplete receiver threads, washers and actual seating position still matter. A deeper head recess changes that bearing position. Check the exact drawing's length-reference convention, especially when changing head style, and keep any load or engagement requirement separate from this stack arithmetic.

Inspect the export and plan a representative check

Open the saved model in the STL Viewer or 3MF Viewer with the intended units and placement. Overall bounds do not measure the bore. A circle through three selected rim points is a construction, not a passage gauge or thread measurement; the bore-measurement guide demonstrates that distinction with existing faceted controls. InspectMesh does not drill, tap or selectively resize the mesh hole. Source CAD owns the local edit.

A representative validation should retain the same nominal size, bore orientation, local wall/boss, material and profile, with the intended hardware. Prusa's modelling guidance describes fit as dependent on geometry, orientation and process conditions (reviewed 1 November 2026); its product-specific figures are not defaults here. No physical assembly was tested for this article.
