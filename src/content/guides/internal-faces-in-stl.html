Internal faces are triangles hidden inside a model, often left behind when two parts were merged badly. You cannot see them from outside, so slice through the model with a cross-section view to find them. Before deleting anything, check the original design: the inside walls of a hollow or channel are internal faces too, and those must stay.

Look inside with a cross-section

Download the plain cube, the cube with a hidden sheet and the block with a channel. The first two look the same from outside. The hidden sheet is two triangles at mid-height, fully inside the cube. The channel block is a 20 x 20 x 10 box with a 6 x 6 mm passage through it.

Open a file in the STL viewer, choose Inspect cross-section, pick Z and slowly move the cut through the middle. Try an X cut through the channel too. The cross-section guide explains the controls. The cut only hides geometry while you look; it does not change the file.

What the mesh report tells you

Example Report What you still have to decide
Plain cube 12 triangles; no holes or non-manifold edges; 1 piece; volume 8,000 Does the outside match the design?
Cube with hidden sheet 14 triangles; 4 open edges (the sheet's border); 2 pieces; volume cannot be calculated Was the sheet a mistake? The report does not say "unwanted".
Block with channel 32 triangles; no holes or non-manifold edges; 1 piece; volume 3,640 Does the channel stay open when sliced?

A loose sheet inside a model usually shows up as an extra piece with open edges. A surface that cuts through another one does not: the edge check does not test for crossing surfaces. The walls of a hollow are a real boundary and can be correct even though you cannot see them from outside.

Keep it or remove it?

Compare with the CAD model and the sliced layers. If a failed merge left a stray sheet, fix the merge in CAD or delete the sheet carefully in a mesh editor. If the faces form a channel or hollow, keep them; if the slicer fills the channel anyway, see slicer fills a hole. Deleting faces without checking can open a hole or change the solid. "Keep largest part" can drop the small sheet in our example, but size alone cannot tell you whether a real inner shell is junk.

Check the edited STL

Keep the original and the edited file. Reopen the edited copy and compare the cross-section, number of pieces, holes, non-manifold edges and the first sliced layers. Do not select faces just because they are small. If the design cannot tell you whether an inner surface is a mistake or a needed hollow, stop and ask the designer.
