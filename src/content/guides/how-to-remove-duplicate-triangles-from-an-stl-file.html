Duplicate triangles are two identical copies of the same face, sitting exactly on top of each other. Removing the extra copy is usually safe and does not change how the model looks. Run a quality check to find them, remove them with a cleanup tool on a copy of the file, then check the copy again.

Why duplicates cause problems

A duplicate makes the edges around it belong to three triangles instead of two, so slicers flag it as non-manifold. Our 13-triangle example cube has one copied face, which creates three non-manifold edges.

How InspectMesh finds them

The STL Quality Checker counts a triangle as a duplicate when its three corners are at exactly the same positions as another triangle's, in any order. The direction the face points does not matter. It skips zero-area triangles here; those are covered in degenerate triangles. Faces that only partly overlap, or that cover the same area with different triangles, are not counted as duplicates.

Try it on the example files

File Result What to do
Clean cube 12 triangles; no duplicates, holes or non-manifold edges Use it for comparison.
Cube with one copied face 13 triangles; 1 duplicate; 3 non-manifold edges Run standard cleanup once you are sure the shape is right.
Cleaned result 1 removed; 12 triangles; no duplicates or edge problems; still 20 x 20 x 20 Reopen it and check the slice.
Copy facing the opposite way 13 triangles; 1 duplicate; 3 non-manifold edges The checker cannot tell which copy is correct. In our test the cleaned output kept the original face.

In both tests, cleanup removed exactly one face and nothing else, and the saved files reopened as 12-triangle cubes of the original size. That is a result for these examples; in a complex assembly, check before deleting.

Remove duplicates in other tools

Prusa's article on corrupted 3D models says PrusaSlicer repairs models automatically "to a certain degree", with "Fix by the Netfabb" available on Windows 10 from the model's right-click menu. Prusa documentation checked 28 November 2026.

When it is not really a duplicate

Two separate parts sharing a face, surfaces that cross each other, or overlapping faces built from different triangles are design questions, not duplicates. A duplicate count of zero does not rule them out. See overlapping surfaces or separate parts; merging solids belongs in your CAD program.

Check the cleaned copy

Run the original and the cleaned file through the quality checker and compare triangle count, duplicates, size, holes, non-manifold edges and flipped faces. Look at a cross-section and the sliced layers where the face was removed. Stop if a hollow, gap or functional surface has changed.
