A nut pocket must do more than match the thread label. Check the nut's outer shape and thickness, how it enters, what stops it rotating and what keeps it in place before the screw is installed. Screw passage and driver access are separate features. A nut trap that fits at its final pose may still have no usable insertion route.

Identify the exact hardware envelope

As one specific reference, Accu HFN-M4-A2, DIN 439 thin hex nut lists a 7 mm across-flats/socket dimension with +0/-0.22 mm tolerance, 2.2 mm thickness with +0/-0.25 mm tolerance, 8.08 mm across points and 0.7 mm thread pitch (reviewed 1 November 2026). These are published facts for this exact product, not measured printed results or a universal M4 pocket specification.

A full-height nut, locknut or another series can need a different envelope and depth. Keep the selected drawing, dimensional tolerances and your own hardware readings separately. A generic M4 hex illustration cannot establish the nut type, corner form or compatibility. This specification reference makes no stock, price or shipping claim.

Separate flats, corners and depth

For an ideal regular hexagon with across-flats dimension F, across corners K = F / cos(30 degrees). With F = 7.00 mm, ideal K is approximately 8.0829 mm. The supplier's rounded 8.08 mm across-points listing is a separate catalogue fact; an actual manufactured outline includes its own tolerances and corner details.

Select a hypothetical pocket F = 7.20 mm only to demonstrate the geometry. Its ideal K is approximately 8.3138 mm. Against an ideal concentric 7.00 mm hexagon, this leaves 0.20 mm across opposing flats, or 0.10 mm per flat in that alignment. A hypothetical 2.50 mm pocket depth against a 2.20 mm nut thickness leaves 2.50 - 2.20 = 0.30 mm nominal axial difference. None of these selected pocket values is a recommended default or a retention mechanism.

Rounded pocket corners, hardware chamfers, offsets and local print error can obstruct insertion even when the flats appear suitable. A circle through three hex vertices measures a constructed circumcircle, not across flats. Use named mesh measurements to describe selected spans and source CAD to define the actual pocket. The round hole/pin method does not automatically size a hex pocket.

Choose the insertion route before enclosing it

A final pocket pose and a usable path to it are separate questions.
Route Check before accepting the design
Top drop-in The opening admits the nut in the intended orientation; the nut can be held while assembling the next part.
Side insertion The complete slot and approach admit the hardware, including corners and thickness, without an obstructing rib or cover.
Enclosed during manufacture The documented manufacturing sequence allows placement and continuation, and the resulting hardware remains where required.

Do not infer a collision-free path from a fitted final pose. This article supplies no print-pause commands or machine-specific insertion procedure. Define that workflow for the actual equipment, then consider whether the nut must be removable or replaceable after assembly.

Separate anti-rotation from retention

Flats can limit rotation once seated. A lip, cover or assembly sequence may keep the nut from falling out before the screw engages. These are distinct requirements: a pocket may resist turning yet release the nut when inverted, or retain it while allowing it to spin. Nominal interference does not establish insertion force, resistance to spin or usable tightening torque.

Choose a diagnostic branch rather than increasing every dimension.
Observed problem Inspect next
Nut will not enter Entrance orientation, corner envelope, local obstruction, pocket depth and direction-labelled physical readings.
Nut enters but spins Actual flat engagement, local shape and movement under the intended assembly conditions.
Nut falls out before assembly The chosen retention feature or sequence; flats alone do not provide axial retention.
Nut cannot be serviced Removal path, access to hold it and the order in which surrounding parts can be removed.

Check screw passage, reach and surrounding material

Confirm compatible thread/pitch, aligned passage axis, projected screw reach and driver approach. Use the screw-role and projection guide for those dimensions, and the head-seat guide for the opposite side. A larger pocket cannot correct an incompatible thread or a screw that bottoms out.

Inspect pocket corners, floor, boss root and nearby edges in source CAD and the reopened export. View the geometry and use clipping to locate expected material/void boundaries. A sampled wall screen is not a nut reaction-load, pull-through or assembly collision calculation.

Compare the insert route and record your validation

A captive nut requires a usable external-hardware insertion and retention arrangement. A heat-set insert instead requires its exact series/length seat, installation method and material applicability. Choose based on access, service and assembly requirements; neither route is declared universally stronger here.

Retain source revision, exact nut reference, pocket/entrance dimensions, intended orientation, local geometry and material/profile with your own measurements. Record whether it entered, remained oriented, stayed retained before fastening and could be replaced. Keep unresolved assembly requirements and the next action alongside those observations. These analytical examples have not been printed or assembled and supply no physical torque or strength result.
