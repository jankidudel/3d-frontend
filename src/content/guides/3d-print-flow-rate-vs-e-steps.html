E-steps or rotation distance relates extruder motor motion to filament feed at the machine. Flow, often called extrusion multiplier, adjusts the requested amount in a material/slicer profile. Establish reliable mechanical feed first, then use an appropriate material-specific test. Do not change either simply because one print has a mark or a tight hole.

Which setting owns the evidence?

Setting Evidence to investigate Boundary
Machine feed calibration Repeatable commanded-versus-actual filament travel under the manufacturer's procedure. Check mechanics first; only adjust supported values for that firmware/hardware.
Material/profile flow A prescribed wall or surface comparison with stable material, temperature, nozzle, width and speed. A profile correction cannot fix drag, slipping or a blocked nozzle.
Another process or geometry choice Speed-dependent gaps, a local transition blob, misplaced seam or directional size error. Locate the cause before treating it as a delivery-calibration problem.

Prusa's extrusion multiplier reference describes its own flow-testing method (reviewed 3 November 2026). Apply your machine/slicer's documented procedure rather than copying another profile's percentage. A baseline of 100% is not proof that every material needs that value.

One controlled testing sequence

  1. Inspect the drive gear, filament path, nozzle and hot end for slipping, drag or blockage. Start from saved known-good values.
  2. Identify the spool, its condition and diameter, then establish a stable temperature using applicable guidance.
  3. Where the manufacturer permits feed calibration, compare a known command with measured filament movement at the prescribed conditions. Repeat before changing a machine value; seek evidence that the difference follows the machine rather than one unstable spool.
  4. After feed is repeatable, run the documented material/profile flow test. Measure several suitable wall positions or evaluate the prescribed surface, rather than selecting a convenient reading.
  5. Change one justified value, retain its previous value, repeat the original control and review walls, tops and dimensions. Revert if the change worsens the control or hides a narrower fault.

Stop if repeated readings vary widely. A partial clog, changing spool condition or slipping filament makes a percentage correction poor evidence. An unusually large proposed correction calls for checking test method, line width and nozzle condition before accepting it. The general calibration sequence supplies the wider prerequisites and return checkpoints.

Retain the arithmetic and its limits

Hypothetical example: a 100 mm command consistently moves 98 mm. The observed command-to-motion ratio is 100 / 98 ≈ 1.0204. That identifies a difference under those conditions, not its cause or a universal firmware update. E-steps and rotation-distance conventions differ; follow the documented adjustment method.

For a suitable single-wall method, the flow calculator applies your entered expected/measured thickness ratio to your current flow. It shows the arithmetic locally; it cannot measure delivery, select the test, diagnose a blockage or write firmware. Transfer a reviewed result deliberately to the relevant profile.

Read symptoms before choosing a correction

Symptom Useful first investigation
Similar under-delivery across materials Feed path, drive gear, nozzle and repeated commanded/actual motion.
One material's walls or tops differ consistently Condition, temperature, effective width and an appropriate profile-flow test.
Gaps only at high speed Applicable throughput evidence, rather than assuming a feed ratio error.
Corner bulges or changing transition blobs Recorded pressure, acceleration, retraction and cooling behaviour with stable delivery.
Stable start/stop mark is in the wrong place Placement around critical faces, separately from extrusion quality.
Repeatable whole-part axis error or local tight fit Axis evidence or local mating dimensions; neither is automatically a flow fault.

Command reading is not delivery measurement

E coordinates, modes and resets describe requested extrusion state; F values describe requested rate. Neither measures feed error, throughput capacity or produced material. Do not change firmware because an isolated E value looks surprising.

Keep printer, firmware/extruder hardware, nozzle, spool condition, temperature, width, speed, test method, raw readings and before/after values together. A correction that improves one surface while degrading dimensions is a reason to investigate and possibly revert. Reliable flow does not itself establish a nominal hole, an invisible seam or a successful assembly.
