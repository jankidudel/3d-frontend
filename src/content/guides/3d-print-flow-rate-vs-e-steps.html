Work out a starting flow value from a single-wall test

Source and further reading

Prusa's official extrusion multiplier calibration guide documents its application-specific flow workflow and explicitly treats it as material/profile fine-tuning. Follow the documentation for your own printer, firmware and slicer rather than transferring one vendor's numeric setting as a universal value.

Final decision rule

Change E-steps only when repeatable commanded-versus-actual feed evidence follows the machine across suitable materials. Change flow only when a stable material/profile test shows a repeatable extrusion-volume difference after mechanics and temperature are sound. Leave both alone when the symptom belongs to speed, pressure, cooling, motion or model geometry. Reprint the original control after any change and keep the smallest correction that improves the intended measurement without degrading dimensions or surfaces.

A calibration order that avoids false fixes

  1. Inspect the drive gear, filament path, nozzle and hot end for drag or blockage.
  2. Confirm the filament diameter and a stable temperature for the material.
  3. Calibrate extruder motion only if commanded and actual filament travel disagree across materials.
  4. Run a material-specific flow test after the mechanical feed is repeatable.
  5. Validate walls, top surfaces and dimensions with the intended slicer profile.

Stop if repeated measurements vary widely. Backlash, slipping filament, heat creep, a partial clog or a changing spool condition makes a percentage correction poor evidence. Fix the unstable cause before calculating another value.

Use repeatable tests

For extruder motion, mark a measured length of filament, command a known feed at a controlled speed, and compare the remaining distance. Repeat before changing firmware. For flow, use the slicer or printer manufacturer's documented method for that material and profile. Measure several walls or evaluate the prescribed surface rather than selecting the most convenient point.

If a 100 mm command consistently moves 98 mm, the observed motion ratio is 100/98, approximately 1.0204. That calculation describes the observed feed difference; it does not prove firmware calibration is the cause. Mechanical checks and repeatability come first.

Common misdiagnoses

  • Using flow to compensate for a partial clog or slipping drive gear.
  • Changing extruder calibration because one wet or unusual filament behaves differently.
  • Using either value to fix corner bulges, seams or pressure-related artifacts.
  • Expecting flow changes to correct a consistent X, Y or Z scale error.

Keep a record of the printer, extruder hardware, nozzle, material, temperature, test method, original value and result. Revert when the new value makes the controlled test worse.

Read symptoms as evidence

Thin, inconsistent lines across every material can point to feed-path or nozzle trouble. A consistent difference on one stable material may justify flow testing. Gaps only at high speed can indicate volumetric-flow limits; rough top surfaces can involve temperature, cooling, infill support or excessive flow. Corner bulges and seam blobs are usually better investigated through pressure, acceleration and retraction behavior than by changing E-steps.

Compare like with like: same model, spool, drying condition, temperature, speed, nozzle and measurement method. Make small changes and keep the old value. If the result improves one symptom but creates dimension or surface problems elsewhere, revert and investigate the narrower cause.

Flow rate and e-steps both affect extrusion, but they are not interchangeable. Treating them as the same can create a profile that works for one spool and fails when material changes.

E-steps or rotation distance: printer-level

This setting describes the relationship between extruder motor motion and filament movement. If it is substantially wrong, every profile starts from the wrong extrusion amount. Follow the process for your firmware and inspect mechanical causes such as a slipping drive gear.

Flow rate: slicer-level

Flow, also called extrusion multiplier, is a small correction in a slicer profile. It accounts for a particular filament, nozzle, line width, temperature and speed. PLA, PETG and different nozzle sizes can need different values.

Which comes first?

First make sure extrusion hardware and machine-level calibration are sane. Then tune flow per material/profile. If a calculation suggests an unusually large change, check line width, nozzle condition and measurement method instead of accepting it blindly.

Neither fixes every fit issue

Correct flow does not guarantee a nominal hole or decide a sliding gap. Use the Tolerance & Clearance Calculator for mating dimensions and the Shrinkage Calculator for consistent whole-model axis error.

Diagnose before changing either value

Symptom Check first
Every material under-extrudes similarly Filament path, drive gear, nozzle and extruder calibration
One material shows thin walls Temperature, moisture, line-width preview and a material-specific flow test
Only corners or seams look wrong Pressure/linear-advance behaviour, acceleration and cooling

E-steps describe commanded extruder travel; flow is a profile-level multiplier used by the slicer. Do not use a flow multiplier to hide a mechanical feed problem, and do not change firmware extruder calibration to correct one unusual filament. Print a controlled single-wall or manufacturer-recommended test, measure several positions, make a small documented adjustment, then repeat.
