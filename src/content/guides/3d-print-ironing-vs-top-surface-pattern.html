A top-fill pattern changes how normal surface paths are arranged. Ironing adds another processing pass over selected eligible surfaces. Identify the face and the problem first: an absent roof, a lost letter, a stepped slope and a change in surface texture require different decisions.

Check that the required surface exists

Compare source geometry and actual paths before choosing a cosmetic operation. For an absent roof use the top-closure diagnosis; for missing strokes or relief use the lettering workflow. Additional top processing cannot recreate omitted source geometry or material paths. A source view and a smooth rendered image alone do not establish that the surface is generated.

Normal top fill and an additional pass are different

Prusa's pattern documentation distinguishes surface-fill choices from internal infill. Its monotonic example changes the ordering of normal rectilinear lines. That is a normal fill-planning choice, not another pass or a universal best-finish ranking.

Prusa's ironing reference describes a second phase over flat tops that includes small extrusion. It is not simply non-extruding travel. The documented scope and edge trade-offs need review for the intended software/profile. This is not sanding or mesh repair, and the page supplies no universal flow, spacing or speed recipe.

Visible top fill, solid cap depth and sparse internal infill answer separate questions. Use walls, caps and core allocation when deciding which region needs material. Changing internal density alone does not select a cosmetic top pattern or guarantee a continuous roof.

Define top relative to print orientation

Locate the face before reviewing process scope
Face Question to inspect
Bed-facing face Its contact and texture are a different condition from an exposed top pass.
Vertical wall Review perimeter paths and start/stop placement rather than assuming top processing applies.
Sloped face Review orientation and layer sampling; a cosmetic pass does not remove all geometric steps.
Flat terrace or raised letter top Verify whether that specific region is selected and receives generated paths.

A face displayed at the top of a CAD image can become vertical or bed-facing after rotation. Compare orientation requirements before choosing a process; the six-direction planner cannot decide which surfaces the native slicer will iron.

A bounded straight-pass calculation

Original hypothetical geometry: a flat rectangle spans X=0..40 mm and Y=0..20 mm, area 800 mm². Parallel straight centrelines span the full 40 mm X width. Their centres sit half a chosen spacing from the Y boundaries. These are explanatory mathematical paths, not recommended settings or generated output.

Only the straight lanes, at an assumed constant 20 mm/s
Spacing s Y centres Lane count Straight length Ideal arithmetic time
0.20 mm 0.10, 0.30, ... 19.90 mm 100 4,000 mm 200 s = 3 min 20 s
0.10 mm 0.05, 0.15, ... 19.95 mm 200 8,000 mm 400 s = 6 min 40 s
  • N = 20 / s: both chosen spacings divide the Y width exactly.
  • L_straight = N × 40 mm: 100 × 40 = 4,000 mm or 200 × 40 = 8,000 mm.
  • t_ideal = L_straight / (20 mm/s): 200 or 400 seconds.

Halving the assumed spacing doubles the counted lanes in this construction. Other widths, angles, boundary insets and islands do not automatically obey this integer formula. The X direction is arbitrary analytical geometry; it does not reproduce PrusaSlicer's documented ironing angle or path generator.

The calculation excludes turns, connectors, travel, acceleration, starts/stops, extrusion, normal top fill and all remaining job operations. It is neither a complete ironing-time prediction nor a universally valid lower bound. No hardware or firmware was run and no material consumption is inferred. Do not transfer 200 or 400 seconds into planning tools as a complete sliced job.

Topmost is not every visible terrace

Consider a hypothetical label with a base terrace at Z=2.0 mm and raised glyph tops at Z=2.6 mm. These heights do not imply a layer count. In the cited Prusa reference, topmost-only scope differs from all-top-surfaces scope: letter tops and the lower terrace need not both be processed. Verify actual coverage in your application/version; do not extend a cosmetic choice to every internal solid layer.

After required paths exist, inspect letter edges, counters, recesses, islands and nearby mating lips. A smoother-looking preview does not prove sharp edges, physical clearance or retained dimensions. The existing lettering controls establish their recorded stroke/relief observations, not a new ironing test.

Choose the first investigation

Observation or requirement Investigate first Do not assume
Different directional sheen on an existing flat top Normal pattern/order and viewing direction. Every change needs an additional pass.
Additional processing on selected flat tops Documented scope, effective profile and generated regions. The whole model or every visible terrace is processed.
Stepped slope Layer schedule and orientation. Ironing removes all stair-stepping.
Absent roof or shallow glyph Source/profile/output diagnosis. A cosmetic operation restores missing paths.
Controlled letter edge or mating lip Actual local paths and relevant physical comparison. An attractive image proves fit or surface roughness.

Compare complete saved jobs and agree the finish

  1. Identify source/revision, selected face and viewing requirement.
  2. Retain the slicer/version, effective normal pattern, additional-operation scope and local overrides.
  3. Compare ironing off/on or another single deliberate change. Record any other settings that changed.
  4. Inspect generated paths around boundaries and small islands, then identify the exact saved output.
  5. Record complete supplied duration and per-material consumption, leaving absent values unknown. Record a physical outcome only after an actual relevant trial.

The G-code checker has no general seam/ironing classifier; recognised motion is not a semantic legend. Review native categories for process intent. After reslicing, use complete totals in the cost calculator, without adding this partial lane example again. Automatic machine passes belong in machine duration; active inspection or hand finishing belongs in separately scoped work.

In the quotation's existing finish and agreed-scope fields, name the visible face, agreed process, protected interfaces and any agreed physical reference/inspection. “Smooth finish” alone defines none of these and certifies no sealing, strength or dimensional result.
