To find the scale percentage, divide the size you want by the size you have and multiply by 100. To make a 40 mm part 60 mm, enter 60 / 40 x 100 = 150%. To halve it, enter 50%.

Common unit fixes

Problem Enter
Designed in inches, imported as mm (25.4 times too small) 2540%
Designed in mm, read as inches (25.4 times too big) 3.937%
Designed in cm, imported as mm (10 times too small) 1000%
Designed in mm, read as cm (10 times too big) 10%

Use these only after you have confirmed the unit mix-up against one dimension you know. A model can also be large on purpose.

The formula

Factor = target / current. Percentage = factor x 100. Change = (factor - 1) x 100. Measure both sizes in the same unit. A "50% increase" means entering 150%, not 50%.

Current to target Factor Enter Change
40 to 60 mm 1.5 150% 50% larger
40 to 20 mm 0.5 50% 50% smaller
508 to 20 mm 1 / 25.4 About 3.937% Inch-to-mm correction

Keep the exact ratio when you apply it; round only what you write down.

Scaling a copy that was already scaled

Always calculate from the file you are about to change. If a 40 mm model was saved at 150%, that copy is now 60 mm. To reach 50 mm from it, use 50 / 60, about 83.33%. Entering 125% would give 75 mm, because 125% was the right value for the original 40 mm file, not the copy. Percentages multiply, so note which file each one applies to.

What scaling changes besides size

  • Area and volume grow faster than length. At 150%, area is 2.25 times larger and volume 3.375 times larger: an 8,000 mm³ part becomes 27,000 mm³. Filament use and print time do not follow exactly the same ratio, because walls and supports behave differently.
  • Holes and clearances scale too. At 90%, a 4.50 mm screw hole becomes 4.05 mm and may no longer fit. If a mounting feature must keep its size, edit the design instead of scaling the whole model. See screw hole sizing.
  • Scaling does not fix the mesh. It keeps the same triangle count and does not fill holes or remove overlaps.
  • Different factors per axis distort shapes. Circles become ovals, so use uniform scaling unless you mean to stretch the part.

Apply it

Enter the percentage in STL Scale or your slicer, save a new copy, then reopen it and check all three dimensions. The STL scaling guide walks through the full process. Scaling does not add a unit to the STL file.
