Support inside a hole or channel is useful only if it can perform its job and be removed without trapping material or damaging the part. Inspect the internal cross-section, slice the actual orientation and trace a removal path before committing to the print.

Separate four different questions

  1. Does the internal surface need support under the chosen profile? A geometry threshold alone cannot answer this.
  2. Did the slicer actually generate support or bridge paths there? Inspect the first affected layers.
  3. Can tools or hands reach the support after printing? A narrow turn or enclosed cavity can block removal.
  4. Will removal damage a mating, sealing or cosmetic surface? Accessibility does not guarantee an acceptable finish.

Compare a through-channel with a narrow-access cavity

The watertight straight-channel control is 40 × 20 × 20 mm with a 40 × 8 × 8 mm passage open along source X. It has 64 triangles, one component and zero open edges. The narrow-access control is a 40 × 30 × 24 mm body with a 26 × 20 × 14 mm chamber reached through an 8 mm long, 4 × 4 mm neck. It has 284 triangles, one component and zero open edges. A watertight surface can enclose an internal void or define a through-channel; “open passage” does not mean “open mesh.”

The InspectMesh cross-section viewer can expose those openings and material boundaries. Clipping does not simulate tool reach, support breakaway force or post-processing damage. Load the control in the local STL Viewer to inspect its clipped geometry before opening the same file in the slicer.

Inspect the support path in the slicer

  1. Orient the model as intended.
  2. Slice with a recorded printer, material and profile.
  3. Locate the first roof/underside inside the channel.
  4. Confirm whether model bridge lines or support/interface lines are generated.
  5. Step through the channel and identify continuous support, gaps and access openings.
  6. Record the intended physical removal direction and smallest opening from source geometry.

In retained preview-only PrusaSlicer 2.9.6 exports, bridge support was allowed with a 45° support threshold, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.45 mm extrusion width, 0.2 mm layers, two perimeters and 15% infill. The straight channel generated support material from Z=0.2 to 10.8 mm and interface paths from Z=4.6 to 11.8 mm. The narrow-access cavity generated support material from Z=0.2 to 16.8 mm and interface paths from Z=4.6 to 17.6 mm. The native slicer preview is authoritative for these labels. The G-code is retained as software evidence, not offered as printer-ready output, and no physical removal was tested.

For a print-in-place moving joint, find support inside the pin/bore gap, beneath retaining shoulders and behind end stops. Identify how a tool reaches it and how removed material leaves; residue can remain trapped in the movement envelope even when its entrance is visible. A support-free preview is not evidence that the physical joint releases. Resolve access before printing rather than relying on a setting name.

Choose a geometry-level alternative

Constraint Geometry-level option to evaluate
Support has a straight removal path Keep the path open and verify tool clearance and critical faces
Support would turn a corner or pass a narrow neck Change orientation, enlarge a deliberate access opening, split or redesign
Internal roof can use controlled bridge paths Test the intended span and profile with a representative coupon
Critical seal or mating face would be contacted Move the interface, split on a safer plane or redesign the surface
Compatible multi-material system is available Follow printer and material guidance for breakaway or dissolvable support

“Support everywhere” is not a complete plan if the support cannot leave the finished part. For a deliberate split, use the split-model workflow. Resin drainage and hollowing are separate decisions covered by the hollowing guide.

Verify the final decision

Retain the source and slicer project; check the internal section; check actual support/bridge paths; verify access/removal direction; protect critical faces; and test a representative section when the full part is costly or important. A geometry control does not prove successful physical removal. Use the broad support-risk guide and bridge comparison for the preceding decisions.
