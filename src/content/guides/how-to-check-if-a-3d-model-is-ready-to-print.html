A model is ready for its next stage when its intended geometry and size are established, relevant findings have a decision, and the remaining slicer and process checks have an owner. A clean mesh report alone cannot answer whether a particular printer, material and setup will produce a usable part.

Choose the next stage from the unresolved question

What is unresolved? Evidence to establish Next stage
File identity or complete geometry Expected revision, required parts and supported package contents. Clarify with the source owner or return to the producing application.
Physical size A known reference dimension and explicit STL assumption or resolved 3MF unit. Measure, then correct an understood export/scale issue on a copy.
Mesh finding The affected region and its intended boundary, connection or surface. Diagnose before selecting a supported correction.
Fit, walls or unsupported regions Usable bed allowances, critical features and generated layers. Inspect the actual sliced job and select orientation/supports deliberately.
Strength, mating fit or service behaviour Relevant material/process evidence and representative measurements or tests. Validate the physical requirement separately from file integrity.

Use the printable preflight record to mark Pass, Review or Unknown and record who will resolve each remaining item. This guide explains the decisions; the checklist owns the compact job record. Do not turn missing information into a pass to finish a form.

Establish the intended object before analysing it

Open the STL or 3MF viewer. Confirm that required parts, openings and placement match the design. A disconnected piece can be an assembly component, and an opening can be intentional. Ask for clarification when intent is missing rather than automatically closing or deleting geometry.

For STL, choose a unit from a known source dimension. For 3MF, inspect the declared unit and the placed build, not just the first resource. Supported component transforms and repeated items can change the overall bounds without adding a second stored mesh. A file extension alone does not establish what a receiving slicer supports.

Use file checks to select a narrow action

Start with STL Print Preflight or 3MF Print Preflight when the concern is unknown. For a specific warning, use the STL diagnostic branches or 3MF investigation. Separate a failed parse from a completed check with findings, and both from completed checks with no listed findings.

The owned closed 20 mm cube has 12 triangles and screened volume of 8,000 mm³. It establishes a small software control, not support strategy or print success. Its missing-face counterpart has four open edges and unavailable volume. That result calls for an intent decision, while a duplicate face calls for a different operation. The saved-copy worked case follows that narrow duplicate correction through to reopened output.

Move from geometry to the actual sliced job

Compare the full footprint, supports and brim with usable bed space. A nominal bounding-box fit does not validate every exclusion region or arbitrary orientation. Use the fit workflow when any axis or allowance is close to its limit.

Review thin features and the first affected layer in the actual material and printer profile. A sampled wall result can miss narrow regions; unmeasured rays and unsampled surfaces stay unresolved. The wall interpretation guide separates geometry width from generated paths and physical requirements. Choosing a better-fitting orientation can also change support access and layer direction, so it needs its own review.

Reopen every changed file you intend to slice. Confirm dimensions, intended shells and affected surfaces. A smaller triangle count or green badge does not establish that a local detail survived. Inspect final layers after repairs, scaling, splitting or reduction rather than carrying forward an old result.

A model with a good mesh report can be ready for slicing review while its fastener requirements remain unresolved. If only a thread label is known, clarify the screw role; if seating or access is uncertain, inspect the head recess or the nut pocket. Do not carry a geometry pass forward as final assembly acceptance. Record the unresolved question in the existing checklist.

Keep physical acceptance and job planning explicit

Define any functional fit, loading, thermal or support-removal requirement with appropriate evidence. The fit and clearance guide explains why a model dimension is not a measured assembled fit. Calibration, adhesion and material preparation remain process decisions.

Once the sliced job is understood, use its complete material and time totals for spool planning and production costing. A solid-model volume is not sliced filament consumption. Retain the source revision, check settings, unresolved findings and next owner with the job. Proceed to that recorded stage only when its prerequisites are known.
