Compare controlled models and sliced paths. slug: embossed-text-not-printing eyebrow: Lettering and sliced detail heading: Embossed or Engraved Text Missing from Your Slice? ---

First check whether the lettering exists in the exported mesh, then find where it changes in the sliced preview. Stroke width and the gaps inside letters are XY features; raised height or engraved depth is a Z feature. Changing one does not automatically fix the other.

This guide compares source geometry and software paths. It does not establish printed readability or physical accuracy. You can follow it without uploading a model.

Find which part of the letter is missing

Symptom First useful check Change to consider
Entire word absent from the exported mesh Reopen the actual STL in the local viewer; inspect body selection and whether the text is solid geometry. Correct the source export. More triangles cannot recreate text that was never exported.
Narrow positive strokes disappear only after slicing Compare local stroke width with the generated model paths under the effective perimeter profile. A local width edit or a tested generator change.
Counter closes or neighbouring strokes join Inspect the negative space through all affected layers. Counter/slot spacing or the relevant profile, rather than a material-wall threshold.
Shallow relief disappears Compare its source Z interval with actual deposition heights, including the first-layer schedule. Relief height/depth or a reviewed layer schedule.
Letter floats above its base Inspect intended attachment and the source union/subtraction. Correct the connection; do not discard every disconnected piece automatically.

Controlled strokes: source widths versus actual paths

The original T1 plaque is 40 x 24 x 2 mm with original polygon O/H/I glyphs. Their strokes are 0.25, 0.45 and 0.80 mm wide, raised from Z=2 to Z=2.6 mm. These are experimental dimensions, not minimum font recommendations. The reopened T1 is one closed 500-triangle shell. In PrusaSlicer 2.9.6, the retained baseline uses a 0.4 mm nozzle, explicit 0.45 mm widths, 0.20 mm layers and first layer, two perimeters, 15% rectilinear infill and no adaptive layers. The complete saved profile retains the other effective settings.

Inspect original T1 STL and corrected T2 STL, or compare their exported surfaces in STL Compare. The exact-output observations retain hashes, regions and heights. Prusa's Arachne documentation explains variable-width controls; this particular result comes from the identified runs, not a universal engine ranking.

Finer layers and wider strokes answer different questions

T3 keeps T2's XY outlines but reduces relief to 0.10 mm, over Z=2..2.1. T3-C has no deposition above Z=2 under the 0.20 mm schedule. T3-F, with both layer and first-layer heights changed to 0.10 mm, has O/H/I paths at Z=2.1: 9, 13 and 4 moves respectively. T2-C's 0.60 mm relief had paths at three deposition heights. These are observed heights, not relief divided by nominal layer height.

Reducing layer height can address this Z representation problem; it does not widen a narrow XY stroke. Match the change to the lost feature rather than globally scaling the entire part, its holes and mounting dimensions.

Read engraved lettering as empty space

T4 subtracts the narrow outlines from Z=1.4..2.0 mm. At deposition Z=1.6, 1.8 and 2.0, the retained Classic paths have zero crossings through the bounded interior strips of the O-left, H-left and I recesses. The O counter instead contains material paths: 62, 63 and 46 crossings in its bounded counter region. That island is attached to the material below the recess. These centreline checks do not measure the eventual groove clearance.

A sampled material-wall ray is not a groove-width measurement. If a void becomes material, use the filled-opening workflow. Reopen every saved correction and inspect all affected sliced layers. InspectMesh does not edit fonts or automatically recognise letter strokes.
