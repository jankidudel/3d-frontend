Choose the slicing mode from the paths and features the object needs. Spiral-vase mode is a continuous contour workflow after its base; ordinary printing can use separate walls, infill and top surfaces. A hollow-looking model and a spiral toolpath are different things. Ordinary closed-loop start/end placement differs from a continuously rising spiral. Do not switch modes just to hide a mark when the part needs ordinary walls, a roof or other incompatible features.

Required result Ordinary slicing Spiral decision
One continuously rising outer contour Ordinary layer changes do not establish this. Inspect actual extrusion Z after the base.
A roof or ordinary infill Can be configured with relevant shell/infill settings. The tested spiral setup disables both.
Two separated upper regions Separate paths can represent them. A successful export alone does not establish both survived.
Designed inner surfaces and a specified cavity wall Inspect those source boundaries and paths. Do not assume a hollow source preserves its inner surfaces in this mode.

One version, three source shapes

The original controls share a 24 x 24 x 30 mm outside envelope. V1 is solid; V2 is an open vessel with a 1.2 mm base and material wall; V3 branches into two upper towers above Z=10. Their material boundaries reopen closed. STL uses an assumed millimetre convention. PrusaSlicer 2.9.6 ran the saved ordinary profile with 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.45 mm explicit widths and 0.20 mm layer/first-layer heights.

The spiral runs deliberately change several effective inputs: two perimeters become one, 15% infill becomes zero, four top solid layers become zero, and spiral mode is enabled. Four bottom layers and support disabled are retained. The complete spiral configuration records the remaining effective settings. This is a mode comparison, not an isolated one-setting experiment.

Actual run Observed paths What follows
V1-N, solid source ordinary Ordinary walls/infill and top-solid paths at Z=30. The source can be sliced with a roof.
V1-S, same source spiral Base paths precede the spiral. All 724 inspected extrusion moves above Z=1 have increasing Z; no upper roof is generated. A closed solid source intentionally becomes an open-top sliced contour.
V2-N, designed vessel ordinary Paths follow inner and outer wall regions; the cavity floor has top-solid paths at Z=1.2. The source cavity is real geometry, separate from infill.
V2-S, vessel spiral attempt Export succeeds. Parsed path coordinates match V1-S exactly under this setup. The designed 1.2 mm inner-wall structure is not retained as the ordinary vessel paths.
V3-S, branching spiral attempt Export succeeds, but above Z=20 paths span X=104.225..111.775 and Y=88.225..111.775: only the right tower is traced. Do not approve the intended two-tower object from successful export status.

Compare V1 solid STL, V2 vessel STL and V3 branching STL in the viewer. The hashed observations identify the exact output and coordinate translation. None of these paths is evidence of leak resistance, adhesion or mechanical suitability.

Choose a source operation or a mode deliberately

If the requirement is only to change internal material, compare ordinary walls, caps and sparse infill first. Setting sparse infill to zero does not itself disable caps or create continuous spiral movement. Choose spiral mode for its actual path requirement, then verify the output.

Use the hollowing guide when the design needs an actual cavity, passage or resin-process opening. Use ordinary walls when the requirement includes separate regions, intended inner surfaces or a roof. A material-wall check does not detect spiral-mode compatibility, and the nozzle planner supplies line arithmetic rather than a slicer verdict.

Prusa's mode documentation describes the named workflow and automatic settings. Match it to your version. When leaving spiral mode, reload the saved ordinary profile and inspect perimeter, infill, top/bottom and support settings; do not assume the toggle restores earlier values. If a roof is unexpectedly absent, use the top-closure diagnosis before changing source geometry.
