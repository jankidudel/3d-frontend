Read the Complete Slicer Total' ---

Slice the intended job with the actual printer and material profile, then use its complete per-material consumption. Check whether supports, brim and purge are included before adding anything. A solid-model mass estimate is a different quantity.

Identify the complete total

Record the source revision, software/profile and actual plate arrangement. Read the report labels: model-only grams, a parts/support subtotal and a complete total are different scopes. Keep consumption and duration per run. Multiply only an explicitly unchanged repeated run; re-slice when quantity or layout changes.

Reconcile included and missing material

If 80 g for parts and supports excludes a separately reported 20 g purge amount, complete consumption is 100 g. If the displayed 100 g total already includes that purge, it stays 100 g. At 24 currency units per 1,000 g, the material costs 2.40 currency units.

These are illustrative calculations, not an observed slicer screen. Use the inclusion reconciliation guide to resolve unclear categories before adding them. Do not add a universal waste percentage to a total already including those components.

Keep each material scope separate

Record each compatible material/tool/spool scope with its grams and price basis. A sum across incompatible materials cannot establish that a single reel will finish the job. Solid-model-equivalent mass uses geometry and density; it omits the actual infill, walls and process structures and cannot replace this sliced consumption.

Why final E is not complete consumption

Absolute E can reset with G92; relative E is incremental. Adding positive E changes can include recovery after retraction and cannot identify complete part, purge and prime consumption across unsupported effects or tools. The last E coordinate and a sum of positive E fields are not complete totals. Follow the worked mode and accounting tables; obtain complete per-material slicer totals when metadata is absent. Unknown is not zero.

Check the available spool

Use the remaining-spool calculator with the matching material, measured gross mass, relevant tare and your explicit reserve. Retain an unresolved tare rather than treating gross mass as usable filament. Reserve is separate from the consumption estimate and a financial failure budget.

Compare settings against the part's requirements

A lower consumption estimate does not prove that the revised part meets its requirements. Re-slice the changed profile and assess the affected walls, supports, layer direction and functional evidence before accepting the saving. Infill percentage alone cannot describe the whole material use.

A lower total can mean a missing required feature

Compare the required object before accepting a smaller estimate. Removing an intended roof can reduce planned material while failing the job's purpose. In the actual PrusaSlicer 2.9.6 R-box runs, the source bytes, nozzle, widths, layer schedule and nonzero infill stay fixed; only top closure is disabled and then restored by reloading the baseline.

R2: same source, top count 0 No top-solid paths; fails a required-roof intention 3.59 g / 1205.33 mm 16m 9s
R3: same source, baseline restored Top-solid paths restored at Z=10 4.35 g / 1460.02 mm 22m 1s

The 0.76 g difference is a comparison of the exported declarations for this single synthetic object and specified profile, not a measured saving or general waste factor. Diameter is 1.75 mm and configured density 1.24 g/cm³, but the grams above are read directly, not recomputed from length. Skirt, brim and supports are disabled. Keep each run's declared scope; unknown categories on another job remain unknown. The exact-file record and roof diagnosis identify the paths and limits. A mode change can change the intended object more fundamentally than a consumption number suggests.

Convert consumption to cost

Use the cost calculator for material, energy, machine use, labour and other production inputs. Use the separate price calculator when you are ready to choose margin or markup and entered selling fees.

For several plates, retain complete consumption and the repeated-run basis in the job allocation record. Do not infer accepted or delivered quantities from planned production.
