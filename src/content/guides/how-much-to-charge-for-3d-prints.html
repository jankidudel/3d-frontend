Build a print quote from the actual work and an explicit profit method. Material, machine time, preparation, finishing, failure allowance and minimum order all need visible assumptions. A calculator can expose cost and margin; it cannot tell you what customers will pay.

Use the editable price calculator with your chosen currency. GBP, USD, CAD, AUD, NZD or EUR labels do not convert prices or identify tax obligations. All figures below are hypothetical currency units (CU), not market rates.

Worked one-part quote

Cost input Hypothetical assumption CU
Material 100 g including supports and purge, at 24 CU/kg 2.40
Machine time 4 hours at 2 CU/hour for your stated machine allowance 8.00
Preparation 20 minutes at 18 CU/hour 6.00
Finishing / inspection 10 minutes at 18 CU/hour 3.00
Failure allowance Chosen fixed 2 CU, not an observed failure rate 2.00
Packing One order, excludes delivery and tax 1.60
Total cost Sum of these assumptions 23.00

With a 30% target margin and no selling fees, price is 23 / (1 - 0.30) = 32.86 CU. A 30% markup gives 23 × 1.30 = 29.90 CU, with a margin of about 23.08%. If the selling channel instead takes 5% of revenue, the 30% margin scenario is 23 / (1 - 0.30 - 0.05) = 35.38 CU. Enter the actual fee rather than treating this as a provider price.

Make the inputs editable and retain the assumptions

  1. Slice the agreed revision, material and orientation; copy complete material and duration estimates.
  2. Set your currency, actual material cost, machine allowance and hands-on labour.
  3. Add delivery, fees and tax treatment explicitly where relevant. Estimates are not invoices or tax advice.
  4. Choose margin or markup deliberately, then apply your stated minimum-order policy.
  5. Export the quote assumptions and retain the file-review report alongside the approved revision.

Allocate setup once when it really happens once

Hypothetical five-part order: production cost is 4 CU per part and one setup/packing charge is 10 CU. Cost is 5 × 4 + 10 = 30 CU, or 6 CU per part. A 30% margin needs 42.86 CU total; a 30% markup gives 39 CU. Five separate runs can repeat unloading and inspection even if preparation was shared.

Failure allowance and minimum order

Use a fixed budget or an observed retry model and document which costs it covers. Do not add an expected failure cost again if the entered machine rate already includes it. With credible comparable-job data, dividing eligible production cost by a success fraction is one scenario; it is not a predicted failure rate for unfamiliar geometry.

A minimum order can cover administration that a small part still needs. In a hypothetical policy with a 15 CU minimum, a calculated 9 CU quote becomes 15 CU; a 32.86 CU quote remains 32.86 CU. State this policy to the customer and avoid counting the same setup twice.

Quote only after intake questions are resolved

Record quantity, exact material/colour, functional dimensions, included design changes, finish, delivery date and quote expiry. A customer-supplied file with unresolved units or missing features needs intake clarification before an unconditional production quote. A clean mesh report does not price support removal or establish material suitability.

Recalculate when quantity, process, revision or orientation changes. Use the production-cost explanation for the underlying inputs, then build your quote using your own values.

Record a quote worksheet after agreeing the work

Use the price calculator and printable worksheet to separate once-per-order setup and packaging from per-part labour and finishing. Choose a currency label, quantity and margin explicitly. Avoid counting failure allowance twice if your machine rate already includes it. The export retains calculation inputs locally until you deliberately download or print it; it is an estimate, not an invoice or production acceptance.
