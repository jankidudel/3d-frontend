Open the saved slicer job and find the first paths intended to touch the bed. If they are missing, check placement, the chosen face and any intended support. If the paths are present but fail as filament goes down, record the location and line shape. Then check the exact build surface, material profile and printer's first-layer procedure. If a corner separates after a continuous base has printed, trace when the corner lifts. These checks apply to filament extrusion printing.

Inspect the first deposited layer in the saved job

Move the native slicer preview to the first deposited layer. Use its feature legend to identify the intended model's paths, any supports and the brim. A purge line or support can touch the plate beneath a raised body. If the body is meant to start on support, record that and confirm the support paths. The PrusaSlicer skirt and brim documentation illustrates why a surrounding skirt and a brim at the first-layer outline serve different purposes. Inspect which paths the saved job actually contains.

Imagine two models with the same 80 × 40 mm XY envelope. One rests on its entire flat underside. The other rests on four separate 10 × 10 mm pads with its spanning underside 2 mm above the plane. At the ideal lower plane the flat model has 80 × 40 = 3,200 mm² of geometric contact. The pads total 4 × 10 × 10 = 400 mm², one eighth as much. This geometric example predicts neither the deposited paths nor how firmly the part will hold. If contact is uncertain, use the orientation planner to compare candidates and the lay-flat workflow to verify the intended face in your slicer.

Match the observation to the next check

First-layer observations and checks
Observation Check next What the check tells you
Expected model paths are missing from the preview Inspect placement, chosen face, intended support and the saved profile. You can locate the discrepancy before a physical print.
Round, separate lines or gaps from the start Follow the maker's nozzle-to-sheet procedure and inspect extrusion consistency. A larger gap can leave loose lines. Insufficient extrusion can also leave gaps.
Ridges, displaced material or apparent scraping Pause or cancel according to the printer's instructions and inspect the sheet and nozzle setup. A small effective gap is possible. Check for surface damage or obstructed extrusion before attempting to improve adhesion.
Continuous lines in one region, repeatable failure in another Check sheet seating, damage and surface preparation, then follow the maker's probing or levelling guidance. A position-dependent symptom gives you a way to check whether a machine-wide adjustment would explain it.
Continuous first layer followed by corner separation Record the first visible lift and follow the corner-lift diagnosis. The comparison now extends beyond first-layer deposition.

Separate nozzle gap, mechanical setup and bed mesh

The initial nozzle-to-sheet gap sets the starting separation. Mechanical setup includes how the sheet sits and how the bed and motion system are aligned. A bed mesh applies position-dependent corrections from sampled heights. Klipper's Bed Mesh documentation describes an approximation whose accuracy depends on the probe and mechanics. Use your printer's instructions for the applicable Z offset, probing or levelling procedure and the installed sheet.

The Prusa calibration photographs for supported i3 printers show round lines and gaps at one extreme and ridges at the other. Use those examples to describe the pattern you see and follow the procedure for your own setup. Check the approved preparation for the exact sheet and filament. Prusa's first-layer guidance distinguishes surface cleaning from separation layers specified for some sheet and filament combinations. A treatment can help release the part and protect the surface.

Record one comparable change

In this hypothetical check, the saved preview shows paths at both approved locations. A small calibration pattern leaves loose lines at left-front and continuous lines at the centre. Repeating the approved pattern at left-front reproduces the loose lines. That makes the affected bed region useful to investigate. Contamination, sheet height, probe repeatability, surface damage and extrusion remain possible explanations.

  1. Record the project revision, exact filament, nozzle, sheet, profile and preparation sequence. Note the first visible symptom and its location.
  2. Save the preview and check which paths and probing region the job uses. If you move the pattern, check what else the new slice changes.
  3. Follow one relevant maker-approved check and repeat a comparable observation. Keep the original and changed observations separate in the calibration record.

If extrusion stays discontinuous above the base, investigate persistent extrusion shortage with the flow versus E-steps guide. If the cooled part seats but is wider near the bed, measure the feature at two heights using the elephant's foot and fit guide.
