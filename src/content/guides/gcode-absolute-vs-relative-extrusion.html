With absolute extrusion, compare a command's E coordinate with the previous logical E position. With relative extrusion, each E word requests a change. G92 E0 resets the logical reference without requesting an ordinary extrusion move. Repeated E1 fields can therefore describe different paths. Establish the firmware, configuration and preceding mode commands before interpreting them.

Separate XYZ positioning from extrusion mode

G90/G91 address positioning; M82/M83 address extrusion mode in the documented contexts. XYZ-absolute movement can coexist with relative E, but command order matters. Marlin documents M82 as selecting absolute E and M83 as selecting relative E; its notes say G90/G91 clear that override. This is named Marlin behaviour, not a promise about all firmware.

The worked tables below use millimetres and non-volumetric E, with initial logical XYZ/E zero and the InspectMesh parser's disclosed state model. They do not describe volumetric E, tool mappings or macro execution. Klipper's command reference and macro state guidance provide separate configuration-specific context. Do not transfer mode-order assumptions between firmware families.

Executing the current InspectMesh parser on the retained files gives two extrusion moves and one travel move for absolute E, versus three and zero for relative E. Both retain recognised linear/extrusion coverage and unavailable duration/material declarations. These are software classification observations, not firmware runs, physical deposition measurements or complete job totals.

G92 changes the reference, not the consumed spool

Marlin G92 documentation describes setting logical position. E-only resets do not request an ordinary extrusion move. G92 XYZ also changes the coordinate frame; relating it to machine-space bounds is a separate question. InspectMesh marks XYZ resets as incomplete machine-space coverage and flags firmware-dependent E transitions, including G90/G91 after explicit E mode and M82 with relative XYZ. Follow incomplete-preview findings rather than clearing a warning because the picture looks familiar.

Positive E includes recovery, not just new part material

Separate hypothetical accounting example: start at logical E=1.00 in absolute millimetre E. E0.40 retracts 0.60; E1.00 restores 0.60; E1.20 advances a further 0.20. Positive deltas sum to 0.80 mm, including recovery of that retract. Net logical advance is 0.20 mm. Neither establishes complete consumption or part mass. Purge, prime, tool changes, volumetric E, firmware retraction, overrides and unsupported effects can introduce further accounting requirements.

If an independently established length is 1,000 mm of 1.75 mm solid filament, at illustrative density 1.24 g/cm³ its approximate mass is π × (1.75 / 2)² × 1000 / 1000 × 1.24 = 2.98255 g. The division by 1,000 converts mm³ to cm³. This original cylinder calculation converts a known length; it cannot recover that length from ambiguous G-code. Use the filament-length converter and the complete consumption workflow for their separate tasks.

Compare the existing examples locally

  1. Open the G-code viewer. It reads locally and sends no commands to hardware.
  2. Use its example selector to load the absolute-extrusion control deliberately. Loading replaces the current file/result; preserve unsaved exports first.
  3. Review per-metric coverage, the solid extrusion and dashed travel paths, then inspect original source lines around G92 in the source evidence.
  4. Load the relative companion and compare the same lines and move counts. No material or duration declaration becomes available merely by changing E mode.
