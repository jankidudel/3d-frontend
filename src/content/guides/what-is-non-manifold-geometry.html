The separate-cube control has two vertex-connected components and no boundary or over-shared edges; whether both parts belong is a design decision. - question: Does a zero boundary count prove one printable solid? answer: No. The crossing-cube control has no boundary edges while its surfaces intersect; the edge test does not identify that crossing. ---

Non-manifold geometry means a surface does not join into one unambiguous neighbourhood everywhere. An edge used by more than two triangles is an easy example. A hole, two separate closed parts and surfaces that cross in space are different observations; they can also cause slicing trouble, but an edge-count result cannot identify all of them.

The three edge patterns

Faces incident on an edge Report meaning What it cannot decide
One Boundary or open edge Whether the opening was intended
Two Ordinary shared edge; direction is checked separately Whether the complete solid is sound
Three or more Over-shared edge, labelled non-manifold edge in the report Which face should be removed

Cases an edge count cannot settle

Downloadable control Implemented checker result Additional question
Open cube 10 faces, four boundary edges Was the square face meant to be present?
Closed cube 12 faces, no edge findings Does the shape match the source?
Duplicate face 13 faces, one duplicate, three over-shared edges Is one exact face redundant?
Separate cubes 24 faces, two vertex-connected components, no edge findings Are two parts intended?
Point-only contact Server vertex connectivity may count one component; edge incidence does not establish a joint Is a physical joint intended? Inspect source CAD.
Crossing cubes 24 faces, two components, no edge findings; intersection not tested Should these solids be joined?

Two separate closed shells can each be well formed and intentionally separate. A point touch can be an invalid joint despite no over-shared edge. Spatially crossing faces can have no shared mesh edge at all. The CGAL mesh-processing manual distinguishes mesh topology from self-intersection testing.

Read your InspectMesh result

The STL Manifold Checker matches exact stored vertex positions, excludes degenerate faces from its edge counts, and connects reported components by shared vertices. The local STL Repair part selector instead needs a complete shared edge. Neither count identifies intended material, nested shells or all vertex non-manifold cases. “Closed by edge test” means no boundary edge was found by that method; it is not a printability certificate.
