--- title: 'Print-in-Place Clearance: Gaps and Moving Parts | InspectMesh' description: 'Check radial, diametral and axial gaps in print-in-place parts, first layers and support access. Separate static clearance from release and movement.' slug: print-in-place-clearance eyebrow: Functional fit and calibration heading: How to Check Clearance in a Print-in-Place Model ---

A print-in-place part needs separation wherever neighbouring bodies must remain free. Check the gap's direction, the first layers, the sliced paths and any support-removal route. A visible gap in the model does not establish that the printed joint will release or move through its intended range.

Identify moving bodies before repair

Mark which bodies must remain separate, which surfaces retain them and which are intended to contact during movement. A captive pin can be essential even when much smaller than the surrounding hinge. Several disconnected shells can be intentional; a component count describes its connectivity method, not whether a mechanism works. Keeping only the largest body or joining everything can destroy the design. Preserve the original before choosing a repair or retained set.

A pin inserted after printing belongs to separate-part alignment. A flexing latch needs deflection and engagement. A housing for separately manufactured hardware belongs to bearing-seat sizing. Here the task is preserving separation in bodies printed in position.

Name diametral, radial and axial gaps independently

Illustrative geometry, not recommended settings or a tested joint. Consider an ideal concentric circular pin and bore. Keep nominal model dimensions separate from intended produced dimensions and observed printed readings.

Hypothetical model dimensions, in millimetres.
Quantity Value or arithmetic Meaning
Pin diameter 4.00 mm Selected model dimension
Bore diameter 4.40 mm Selected model dimension
Diametral gap 4.40 - 4.00 = 0.40 mm Total difference across diameters
Ideal radial gap 0.40 / 2 = 0.20 mm Each side only when concentric
Selected axial face gap 0.25 mm Spacing of two identified opposing end faces, independent of diameter

If there are two end gaps, identify each separately; the 0.25 mm value does not silently apply to both. A pin displaced toward one side no longer has 0.20 mm on both sides. Retaining shoulders, noncircular geometry and facet flats can govern the tightest region. A single nominal gap cannot describe every surface in the joint.

Both printed interfaces can consume the gap

Now assume hypothetical bore error -0.12 mm and pin error +0.08 mm, with error defined as produced minus modelled size. These are invented inputs, not measurements made by InspectMesh.

A separate hypothetical produced result.
Quantity Arithmetic Result
Produced bore 4.40 - 0.12 4.28 mm
Produced pin 4.00 + 0.08 4.08 mm
Produced diametral gap 4.28 - 4.08 0.20 mm
Ideal concentric radial gap 0.20 / 2 0.10 mm
Produced axial gap No axial error supplied Unknown

The nominal 0.40 mm diametral gap has halved under those assumptions. The radial result cannot infer the produced axial spacing. For dimension planning use Both parts printed in the tolerance calculator: it uses your target produced pin diameter and separate local errors. Its target is not automatically the 4.00 mm nominal model input above. Keep chosen clearance and calibration distinct; the calculator does not assess release, supports or motion.

Inspect sections along the whole interface

  1. Open the exact revision in the STL Viewer or 3MF Viewer. Confirm STL unit interpretation or resolved 3MF units and placed-item scope.
  2. Follow entry, middle, exit, shoulders and end stops with the visual clipping controls. Hiding a surface changes the view, not the exported geometry.
  3. Compare source dimensions and retain selected mesh references. A three-point circle cannot locate the tightest passage; arbitrary point distance is not automatically normal face clearance.
  4. Check other levels and poses in source CAD. A positive gap at one section is not a global minimum or a swept-motion check.

Follow the receiving slicer's layers

In your actual printer/material profile, step through the interface from its first layer to the retaining region. Look for distinct intended contours, unintended connecting paths, seam locations at moving interfaces, bridge/sag regions and generated support within the movement envelope. Keep the source gap, generated transition and observed physical release as separate evidence; a placement setting guarantees neither movement nor a global minimum clearance. These are reader inspection steps, not a newly observed slicer experiment. A path preview describes that generated job; it does not fully predict deposited shape.

Check the bed-facing gap separately

First-layer widening can close a moving interface while the regions above remain separated. Compare the location of binding with source geometry and actual layers before changing every wall. Prusa describes the bounded purpose of elephant foot compensation (checked 2 November 2026). A profile-specific adjustment or source chamfer does not solve every joint problem. Follow the base-interference investigation and recheck small features and adhesion after a justified change.

Check whether the preview already joins the paths across the intended gap. If the paths are separate, compare the actual base and upper gap. A local base flare and a corner that lifts later and brings the bodies together call for different changes.

Plan support removal before printing

Locate any support generated between pin and housing, under a shoulder or behind an end stop. Identify an opening and a direction through which tools and removed material could leave. A narrow captive gap can trap support or residue that blocks movement. Use the internal support-access workflow; settings alone do not establish access or removability. Consider orientation, deliberate access geometry or printing separately when removal cannot be planned. No support in a preview still does not prove release.

Distinguish release from useful movement

The initial pose, intermediate positions and endpoints can have different interference. Review the motion in source CAD where possible, including shoulders and end stops. InspectMesh provides no swept-volume, friction, wear, force or lifetime analysis. A joint that releases once can still bind later in its travel or have unsuitable play.

Record one change and its outcome

If you test a representative joint, retain revision, orientation, process and profile, each gap's direction and basis, whether it released, and where in its travel it bound. Keep unsuccessful variants and change one justified input before comparing again. No such physical tests were performed for this guide. Prusa's modelling guidance describes fit as dependent on geometry, orientation, calibration, settings and material (checked 2 November 2026). There is no universal clearance value; intentional separate bodies and a viewer image alone cannot establish movement.
