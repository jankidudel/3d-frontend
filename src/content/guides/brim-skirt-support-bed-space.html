A model-only fit does not include every structure the slicer may add. Record the usable bed region, the spacing between parts and any extra footprint separately, then inspect the actual generated plate before accepting the layout.

Separate three different spaces

What the planning controls mean
Basis Meaning What remains unknown
Model envelope Full projected XY bounds in the chosen orientation, with Z checked separately. First-layer contact, exact outline and generated structures.
Bed edge exclusion Space removed from each rectangular edge, or radially from a circle. Irregular or height-dependent machine restrictions.
Extra XY allowance per part A chosen expansion on all four sides of each model envelope. Whether actual supports, brims or remote structures stay inside it.
Inter-part spacing The selected gap between already expanded planning rectangles. Head/gantry clearance, removal access and print reliability.

The bed layout planner uses these independent controls. Its initial 5 mm edge exclusion and 5 mm gap preserve the earlier rectangular planning example; zero extra expansion is the starting assumption. Those values are editable examples, not universal printer requirements. If you already entered a reduced usable diameter or span, do not subtract its exclusion twice.

Work out the remaining gap

Take two hypothetical 100 x 80 mm body envelopes with a body-to-body gap of 5 mm. A hypothetical 4 mm expansion on each facing side leaves 5 - 4 - 4 = -3 mm. The reserved regions overlap by 3 mm even though the model bodies do not touch. To leave 5 mm between those expanded rectangles, the corresponding body gap must be 4 + 5 + 4 = 13 mm.

In the planner, set Extra XY allowance per part to 4 mm and Inter-part spacing to 5 mm. It reserves the expansion first, then separates the reserved rectangles. Do not add another 8 mm to the gap field for the same two expansions. Actual brim separation, object-specific settings and support outline still need the generated preview.

Check expansion against the usable span

On a hypothetical 220 mm rectangular bed, an additional 5 mm exclusion per edge leaves 210 mm. A 200 mm-wide body with 6 mm expansion on each side requires 200 + 2 x 6 = 212 mm. It exceeds the remaining span by 2 mm. Reducing only the gap between neighbouring parts cannot fix a single expanded part that exceeds the boundary.

For a circle, compare all expanded envelope corners with the effective disc. Individual X/Y spans below the diameter do not establish containment. The round-bed fit guide owns the diagonal, corner slack and placement mathematics. Changing model scale does not automatically reduce a fixed physical allowance: reserve the same selected millimetres after scaling the body.

Generated structures need their own review

A per-object brim and a skirt around a group do not occupy the same region. A support base or raft can be asymmetric. A separate wipe or purge structure may be remote from every model envelope. Start routines can add motion elsewhere. A single uniform expansion cannot establish the combined footprint of these different structures.

Prusa's Skirt and Brim documentation distinguishes skirt loops and object distance from brim width and separation. It also documents object-level brim overrides in Advanced/Expert modes (reviewed 1 November 2026). Check both global and per-object settings in the intended project; a global value alone may not describe a particular object's generated outline. This source supports those control distinctions, not a footprint we have sliced or a universal adhesion width.

Use the intended material, machine and process requirements to select the actual structures. Do not suppress manufacturer exclusions or reduce a needed adhesion setting solely to force the planner's rectangle inside a boundary. If a process requirement consumes the available space, revise orientation, quantity or the job plan.

Interpret the working layout suggestion

Choose a confirmed saved profile or explicitly switch to editable session settings. Saved centimetre and inch dimensions are converted to millimetres. Circle is a deliberate shape choice. Review any older ambiguous axis record before saving; the original Width/Height/Depth values do not tell the software which is physical Z.

Rectangle mode uses a shelf heuristic. Circle mode tries centred, neighbouring and bounded grid positions with optional 90-degree turns. Accepted placements pass the declared region and spacing checks, but the finite search does not find every possible arrangement. Not placed by this layout is a reason to try another arrangement or review the native slicer, not proof of packing impossibility or a required second plate.

The numbered diagram and placement table distinguish model envelopes from expanded reservations. Planner X/Y coordinates describe that diagram, not firmware coordinates. The displayed area percentages divide accepted model or reserved rectangle area by the effective usable region. They do not measure bed contact, deposited material or optimal packing density.

Review the whole generated job

  1. Apply the intended placement, printer profile and object-specific settings in the native slicer.
  2. Generate supports and adhesion structures, then inspect their actual first-layer and full-height extents.
  3. Review separate purge/wipe structures and relevant start/end routine paths against the documented usable region and origin.
  4. For one-at-a-time printing, perform the separate head, gantry and print-order review. Rectangle spacing and circular containment do not model those collisions.
  5. Keep the native project and resolve any incomplete path coverage before relying on a G-code coordinate observation.

Record accepted quantity separately from material and time

After reviewing the suggestion, explicitly confirm the accepted placement count before offering it to the cost calculator. The offered number represents accepted unsliced planning placements, not every uploaded part or successful production. Changing parts, shape, dimensions, units or allowances invalidates that confirmation.

Use complete sliced consumption and duration for the production-cost workflow. Bed occupancy cannot determine grams, supports or print time. Keep separately absent material explicit, preserve the original job assumptions and apply the offered count deliberately at the destination.
