An STL volume is usually wrong for one of four reasons: the wrong unit (an inch model read as millimetres is 16,387 times off in volume), holes in the surface, faces pointing the wrong way, or several overlapping parts. Check the unit first, then the mesh. InspectMesh leaves the volume blank when the mesh is not a clean closed solid, rather than showing a misleading number. Blank never means zero.

Unit and scale errors

Our 20 mm example cube shows how much the volume moves:

What you do with the cube Side length Volume
Open it as millimetres 20 mm 8,000 mm³
Open the same file as inches 508 mm 131,096,512 mm³ (8,000 x 25.4³)
Save a 150% copy, open as mm 30 mm 27,000 mm³ (8,000 x 1.5³)
Remove one side, open as mm 20 mm Blank, not zero

Volume grows with the cube of the size, so small unit mistakes become huge volume mistakes. Check one known dimension first using the example cube and the STL units guide.

Negative volume and flipped faces

Volume is calculated by adding up a small signed piece for every triangle. If every face of a closed part points inward, the total comes out negative. That only means the faces are reversed, not that the part has negative material. If only some faces are flipped, pieces can cancel out and give a number that is simply wrong, so fix the faces rather than just dropping the minus sign. See fixing flipped normals.

InspectMesh's collection check first looks for holes, zero-area and duplicate triangles, flipped faces and the number of separate parts. It only reports a volume for a single closed part with consistent faces, and never shows a negative volume. Several parts give a blank result on purpose. It does not check for overlapping surfaces, so a volume still does not prove the solid is correct.

What you see Check What to do
Blank volume Holes, zero-area or duplicate triangles, flipped faces, parts Fix the problem found; never replace it with zero
Far too big or small One known dimension and the unit you chose Fix the unit once you are sure which one is right
Negative in another program Whether the faces point inward Fix the face direction
Zero or tiny for a real model Open surfaces, flipped patches, overlaps Go back to CAD if the solid is unclear
Several overlapping parts Whether they should be merged or keep a hollow Don't add their volumes together blindly

A designed hole is not a missing face

Note which tool gave you the number: the browser collection check and the server checker use slightly different rules, so they may not both show a volume even when they agree on the size.

Example (mm) Expected What both InspectMesh checks report
Closed 20 mm cube, 12 triangles 8,000 mm³ No open edges; about 8,000 mm³
Cube with one side missing, 10 triangles Not a closed solid 4 open edges; volume blank
40 x 30 x 10 mm block with an 8 mm square hole through it 12,000 - 640 = 11,360 mm³ No open edges; about 11,360 mm³

Download the block with a hole and the cube with a missing side. Results from 27 October 2026: browser check and server checker code run locally. A designed hole is empty space with walls all round it; a missing face is an unfinished surface. These are software results, not print tests.

For a hollow model, see how to hollow an STL and look inside with a cross-section. Don't work out a hollow by subtracting volumes of separate shells.

After fixing it

  1. Keep the original and note its size and the unit you used.
  2. Run STL preflight to find the actual problem.
  3. Fix it in CAD, or fix a clear mesh error on a copy.
  4. Compare the two versions and check known dimensions, not just the volume.
  5. Check the solid and the sliced layers in your slicer.

Model volume is not filament use

Volume treats the model as solid. A real print uses walls, infill, supports and purge, so it can need much less (sparse infill) or more (supports). For quotes or spool planning, use the total from your slicer. See how much filament a print uses.

The InspectMesh tests check closed and open example cubes and inch scaling. For comparison with 3MF files, which declare their unit, see the 3MF Core Specification (reviewed 7 October 2026). These are software and arithmetic results, not physical measurements.
